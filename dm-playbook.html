Paid inbound · DM ads · v2

Every lead in this inbox cost money.

This is the system for converting inbound DM ad leads — people who clicked a paid ad, triggered a keyword, or hit "Send message" and landed in your inbox cold. They behave nothing like a story reply: they arrive in bursts, at all hours, with no relationship and a countdown running. Work them accordingly.

Core

The ad buys attention. The DM buys the call. The call sells the coaching. Three separate jobs — do not collapse them. The most expensive mistake in paid DM is pitching coaching to someone who clicked an ad forty seconds ago, because you burn a lead you paid for at the exact moment they were least ready.

The second most expensive is being slow.

01

How paid leads differ — and the ten rules

If you have set DMs before off organic traffic, most of your instincts are about to work against you. Read this section carefully before anything else.

Organic leadInbound ad lead
RelationshipFollows you, has seen your contentNone. May not know your name.
IntentMixed, often highImpulse. Two-second decision on a scroll.
First messageSomething they wroteA keyword or canned icebreaker. Carries no information.
ArrivalTrickle, wheneverBursts, tied to ad delivery. Often overnight.
ExpectationMight reply tomorrowExpects a reply in minutes, forgets you in hours.
Follow-upOpen-endedCapped by a platform messaging window. See section 02.
Cost of losing oneTimeTime and cash. You already paid for it.
RULE 01 Five minutes, not five hours. A paid lead's intent decays faster than anything in this business — they were mid-scroll and have already moved on. Reply inside five minutes during ad hours and the same conversation is a different conversation. This single variable moves more revenue than any script on this page.
RULE 02 Deliver what the ad promised, first, with no strings. If the ad offered a guide, a meal plan, a free week — send it immediately, before any question. They came for that thing. Holding it hostage behind a qualifying question is the fastest way to be reported, blocked, and to tank the ad account's engagement quality.
RULE 03 Know which ad they came from before you type. The hook they responded to tells you their goal, their language and their frame. Someone who clicked a postpartum ad and someone who clicked a strength ad need entirely different first messages. If your setup doesn't tag the source, fix that before you scale spend.
RULE 04 They don't know who you are. Say who you are. Organic leads arrive knowing you. Ad leads genuinely do not. One short line of identification in the first human message prevents half the "who is this?" and "is this a bot?" confusion that kills paid threads.
RULE 05 One question per message. Two questions means they answer the easy one. On a cold paid lead who owes you nothing, it often means they answer neither.
RULE 06 Never pitch in the first human message. Not even softly. They clicked an ad, not a sales page. Pitching immediately confirms the fear they had when they clicked — that this was bait — and it is the number one driver of "reported as spam", which quietly raises your costs across the whole account.
RULE 07 Get permission before you offer. "Can I ask you something?" · "Want my honest take?" A yes before the offer turns a push into a pull. On cold paid traffic this beat matters more, not less.
RULE 08 Get off the platform early. Once there's a real conversation, get an email or a phone number with their consent. The messaging window closes; your email list doesn't. A lead you can only reach inside a 24-hour window is a lead you will lose on day three.
RULE 09 Only say what the ad can back up. No guaranteed results, no medical claims, no promises the ad didn't make and you can't keep. Advertising standards apply to what you say in the DM too, and a mismatch between ad and DM is both a compliance problem and a refund waiting to happen.
RULE 10 Every dead lead is data. Log why. Wrong country, price shock, only wanted the freebie, under 18, wrong goal — these are not just lost deals, they're an ad targeting brief. Section 14 covers how to feed it back.
02

The messaging window

The single biggest structural difference between paid DM and organic DM, and the thing most coaches discover only after they've lost a month of leads. Understand it before you build any follow-up habit.

Messaging platforms limit how long a business can keep messaging someone after that person's last message. On Messenger and Instagram this is commonly a 24-hour standard messaging window that reopens every time they reply. Outside it, business-initiated messages are restricted, and sending through an automation tool may simply fail or put your account at risk.

Two things decide how much this constrains you:

  • If you reply manually from the app — the way most coaches actually work — you have more practical latitude, but the spirit still applies: a cold paid lead who hasn't replied in four days does not want a fifth message.
  • If you use an automation platform (ManyChat and similar), you are on the API and the window is enforced. Some message tags — a human-agent tag, for instance — extend it, and your platform will tell you which it supports.
Verify

Platform messaging policies change, and they differ by channel and by country. Confirm the current rules for the exact channel you run ads on before you build your follow-up sequence around them — don't take this page's word for it a year from now.

The practical consequence: your qualifying, your transition and your booking should all happen inside the first conversation wherever possible — not spread across three days the way an organic lead can be. Aim to get from their first message to a booked call in one sitting.

The escape hatch

Because the window will close on a chunk of your leads, capture a channel you control while the conversation is live. Ask naturally, at the point it makes sense, and be straight about why.

Channel capture · mid-conversation

What's the best email for you? DMs bury things and I'd rather not lose you in the pile — I'll send [the guide / the times / the details] there too.

Consent

An email given so you can send one specific thing is not consent to add them to a marketing list. If you're going to market to them, say so and get proper opt-in — data protection rules where you operate will have a view on this, and the fines are not theoretical.

03

The six stages

Numbered because the order is load-bearing. Stage 2 is the one that doesn't exist in organic DM and the one coaches skip — skipping it is why paid leads ghost.

1

Respond

Goal: a human reply from you, inside five minutes, that says who you are.

Target: <5 min in ad hours Messages: 1 Exit when: sent

Their first message is almost always a keyword or an icebreaker the ad put there — "INFO", "Send me the guide", "I'm interested". It tells you nothing about them except which ad they clicked. Treat it as a doorbell, not a conversation.

Your reply does three things in about fifteen words: confirms a human is here, names who you are, and sets up the delivery.

Do
  • Reply fast, even if the full delivery takes another minute
  • Name yourself and the business once
  • Reference the ad's actual offer in their words
  • Keep it to one or two lines
Don't
  • Open with a qualifying question before delivering
  • Send a wall of text about your programme
  • Make them repeat what they already clicked
  • Let a bot carry the whole thread — see section 10
2

Deliver

Goal: hand over exactly what the ad promised, with nothing attached.

Messages: 1 Time: immediately Exit when: they have the thing

This stage exists only in paid inbound, and it is non-negotiable. They clicked because of a specific promise. Deliver it in full, for free, without asking for anything — then and only then do you have the standing to ask a question.

Delivering properly does three jobs at once: it keeps the promise your ad made, it proves you're real, and it creates the small reciprocity that makes the next question feel reasonable rather than intrusive. Coaches who gate the freebie behind a question lose a meaningful share of paid leads right here, and those leads are the ones most likely to report the ad.

Never

"Sure — just answer a couple of questions first and I'll send it over." You advertised a thing. Send the thing. If your offer only works as bait, the offer is the problem, not the lead.

3

Qualify

Goal: goal, history, real obstacle, timeline — plus whether they're even in your market.

Messages: 4–8 Time: same session ideally Exit when: you can state their problem back

Same GAPT framework as organic (section 05) with one addition: paid traffic includes people your targeting reached by accident. Confirm location, age bracket if it's relevant to your offer, and that they're actually looking for coaching rather than a free download. Disqualifying fast is a skill — every minute on a dead paid lead is a minute off a live one.

Exit test: can you write one sentence describing their situation that would make them say "yeah, exactly"? If not, ask another question.

4

Transition

Goal: permission to talk about working together.

Messages: 1–2 Time: straight after qualifying Exit when: they say yes to hearing more

Two beats — reflect, then ask permission. On cold paid traffic the reflect beat is doing double duty: it's also the first moment they think "this person actually listened", which is rare enough on an ad-driven thread to be a genuine differentiator. Full scripts in section 06.

5

Book

Goal: a specific time, ideally within 24–48 hours, with contact details captured.

Messages: 2–3 Book within: 48 hrs Exit when: invite sent + confirmed

Book paid leads sooner than organic ones. An organic lead who booked nine days out still knows who you are next week; a paid lead often doesn't. Two concrete times, never "when are you free", and always take the phone number — it's your route back in when the messaging window shuts.

6

Confirm & show

Goal: they turn up. Paid leads no-show more than organic ones. Plan for it.

Touches: 4 Channels: DM + email/SMS Exit when: the call happens

A lead who met you forty minutes ago via an advert has far less social obligation to show up than someone who's followed you for a year. That's not a character flaw, it's just the nature of the source — so the confirmation sequence in section 07 is longer and runs on two channels.

04

Openers by ad type

What you say depends entirely on what the ad said and how they arrived. In every case the shape is the same: identify yourself → deliver the promise → one easy question.

A. Click-to-message ad with an icebreaker

They tapped "Send message" on an ad and the platform pre-filled a prompt. They may not have typed a single word themselves.

Ad hook: "the 3 mistakes keeping you skinny-fat"

Under 5 minutes. Names a human. No question yet.
[the guide]
That's yours to keep. Out of curiosity — which of the three was the one that made you click?
Delivered first. The question is about the content, not about them buying.
honestly the second one, I train hard but my diet is all over the place
That's stage 3 open, and it took four messages.
Click-to-message · respond

Hey! It's [your name] here from [business] — sending that over now 👇

Click-to-message · deliver + open

[the thing you promised] That's yours, no strings. Quick one — what made you click on it? Is [the ad's topic] the thing you're wrestling with at the minute?

B. Comment-to-DM keyword automation

They commented a keyword on an ad or post and got auto-DM'd. Highest volume, lowest average intent — a lot of people comment reflexively. Expect a big drop-off between the auto-message and a real reply, and don't read it as failure.

Keyword: "RESET"
Hey! Here's the 7-day reset you asked for 👇 [link]
thanks!
No worries — [name] here by the way, real person 👋 Did you grab it because you're coming back from a break, or just trying to tighten things up?
Human takes over immediately after the bot delivers. Binary question — easy to answer one-handed.
Keyword · human handover

No worries — [your name] here by the way, real person 👋 Did you grab it because you're [situation A], or more [situation B]?

C. Lead-form ad, then DM

They filled in a form, so you have a name and contact details but no conversation. You're initiating — so reference the form explicitly or it reads as cold outreach.

Lead form · first DM

Hey [name] — [your name] from [business]. You put your details in about [the offer] earlier, so I thought I'd message here rather than just email you. Sent it over to [their email] as well. Did it land alright?

D. Retargeting ad — they've seen you before

Warmer. They've engaged with something of yours already. Acknowledge it lightly without being creepy about how you know.

Retargeting · respond

Hey! [your name] here. Sending that over now — and good to properly say hello, I think you've been around the page a little while 👋

E. Story ad / swipe-up reply

They replied directly to a story ad, often with a reaction or a couple of words. Closest thing to an organic story reply in the paid world — treat it as the warmest paid lead you'll get and go slightly more conversational.

Story ad · respond

Ha, appreciate that — [your name] here 👋 Is that something you're dealing with right now, or were you just nodding along?

Always

Whatever the ad type: identify yourself in the first human message. "It's [name] here" costs four words and removes the single most common friction point on cold paid traffic — the quiet suspicion that they're talking to a bot or a scam.

05

Qualifying: GAPT, plus the fit check

Four things you need before you offer anything, asked one message at a time with a human reaction in between. Then a fast fit check, because paid targeting is imprecise and some of these people were never in your market.

G — GOAL What are they actually trying to do? "What are you working towards at the minute?" · "If this went perfectly, what's different in six months?" — On ad traffic you often get the ad's language back ("I want to get toned"). Push once for something concrete: a number, an event, a feeling they can describe.
A — ATTEMPTS What have they already tried? "What have you tried so far?" · "What's worked best of everything you've done?" — Separates the never-started from the been-burned, and it's the best single predictor of how to pitch on the call.
P — PAIN Where does it actually break down? "What's the bit that keeps tripping you up?" · "Where does it usually fall apart — training or food?" — People rarely lack information. They lack structure and a plan that survives a bad week.
T — TIMELINE Now, or someday? "Is this a 'starting now' thing or more of a 'thinking about it' thing?" — The most skipped and most valuable question, and on paid traffic it's essential: a large share of ad clickers are in browsing mode and will happily chat for twenty minutes without ever intending to start.

The fit check — 30 seconds, saves hours

Broad targeting reaches people you can't serve. Confirm these early and naturally, woven into conversation rather than fired off as a checklist.

  • Location / timezone — "Whereabouts are you based?" Catches targeting leak and tells you whether your call times work.
  • Age bracket — only where your offer requires it. Under-18s do come through ads. See play 35.
  • Are they after coaching, or the free thing? — the T question usually surfaces this on its own.
  • Anything that puts them outside your scope — injury, medical condition, pregnancy. Plays 36 and 37.

Reading the answers

Green — go now, in this session

Specific goal. A deadline or trigger event. Has tried alone and it hasn't worked. Describes the pain unprompted. Asks how you work. In your service area.

Action: transition immediately — same conversation, do not "let them read the guide and come back". The window is open now and it will not be open on Thursday.

Amber — one more angle, then park

Vague goal, no timeline, friendly but non-committal. Engaged with the freebie but not with the idea of changing anything.

Action: one genuine value message and one honest question (play 16). If it doesn't move, capture an email, put them on the ladder in section 09, and get back to live leads. Do not spend an hour here while five new leads sit unanswered.

Red — close it kindly and log why

Outside your service area. Only ever wanted the download. Can't afford it under any structure you actually offer. Needs a clinician. Under 18 without a guardian. Signs of disordered eating (play 36). Obvious spam.

Action: be warm, be quick, end it. Then log the reason — red leads are the raw material for fixing your targeting, and that's worth more than the lead was. Section 14.

06

The transition

Two beats, in this order, never skipped. On paid traffic this is where a conversation either becomes a pipeline or becomes a polite goodbye.

  1. Reflect. Say their problem back in your own words, accurately. You're proving you listened — which, to someone who arrived via an advert and half-expected a bot, lands harder than you'd think.
  2. Ask permission. A small, easy yes that opens the door. Never offer without it.
The transition, in full
yeah I'll be good for 3 weeks then one bad week and I've completely fallen off again
So it's not that you don't know what to do — it's that nothing's holding it together when life gets in the way. Sound about right?
Beat 1: reflect.
yes that's exactly it
That's honestly the main thing I fix for people. Can I ask — are you open to having someone coach you through it, or are you set on figuring it out solo?
Beat 2: permission. The "or solo" option is real — let them take it.
no I'm open to it, been thinking about it for a while
Cool. Easiest thing is a quick 15 min call so I can get the full picture and tell you straight whether I can help. No pitch, and if I'm not the right fit I'll say so. Want me to send a couple of times?
Beat 1 · reflect

So if I've got this right — it's not that you [the thing they clearly can do], it's that [the actual breakdown point]. Is that fair?

Beat 2 · permission

That's pretty much exactly what I help people with. Can I ask — are you open to having someone coach you through it, or are you set on doing it solo?

The ask

Easiest thing is a quick 15 minute call so I can hear the full picture and tell you straight whether I can help. No pitch, and if I'm not the right fit I'll tell you. Want me to send over a couple of times?

Why

"If I'm not the right fit I'll tell you" is the most important line in the script — and it only works if you actually do it. Say it, mean it, and turn people away on calls when it's true. On paid traffic especially, prospects arrive braced for a pitch; being the one person who isn't pitching is the whole edge.

07

Booking and getting them to show

Paid leads book later and show less than organic ones. Both are fixable, mechanically, and this is the cheapest revenue in the whole system — a no-show is a lead you fully paid for and got nothing from.

Two times, close in, and take the number

A booking link is a hurdle and "when are you free?" is homework. Two specific times is a three-second decision. Make them oddly specific — 7:10, 12:40 — it reads as a real calendar rather than a sales grid. And book close in: inside 48 hours wherever you can.

Booking · two-option close

I've got [tomorrow 7:10pm] or [Thursday 12:40pm] — which suits you better?

Booking · capture the channel

Perfect. Best number to reach you on, and an email for the invite? DMs bury things and I don't want this getting lost.

Do not skip

Taking the phone number and email is what lets you confirm and re-book after the messaging window closes. On paid traffic it is the difference between a bookings calendar and a wish list. Get it every time, before you send the invite.

Four show-up touches, across two channels

Touch 1 · immediately · DM + email

Invite, expectations, one prep question

Makes it real, makes it short, and gets a small commitment that makes skipping it feel rude. The prep question is doing the heavy lifting — a lead who answers it shows up far more often.

Sent the invite over 👍 It'll take about 15 minutes — I'll ask about your training and where it's getting stuck, then tell you honestly whether I can help.

One thing before we talk: what would need to come out of the call for it to be worth your time?
Touch 2 · night before · DM

The soft confirm

Short, one question, gives them a graceful way to move it rather than ghost it. Their reply also reopens the messaging window.

Hey [name] — still good for 7:10 tomorrow?
Touch 3 · 1 hour before · SMS

The practical reminder

Sent by text, not DM — it cuts through, and it works regardless of what the messaging window is doing.

Calling you in an hour on this number. Somewhere you can talk properly? — [name]
Touch 4 · at the time · call

Ring them anyway

Silence on touches 2 and 3 is not a cancellation. A meaningful number of paid leads simply don't check DMs and will answer the phone. Not answering is a no-show — handle it with play 41.

Play 01 · Ad leads

They trigger the keyword, get the freebie, and never reply again

What it usually means

They wanted the free thing. This is the single highest-volume outcome in keyword-automation campaigns and it is normal — a large share of any keyword campaign never has a real conversation. It is not a failure of your scripts.

Do this

One human follow-up inside a few hours, framed around the content rather than around them buying. If nothing, one more the next day, then park them.

Freebie follow-up · same day

Did that make sense? There's one bit in there people always get stuck on — happy to explain it properly if you got that far.

Then

Log it as freebie-only. If a given ad produces mostly these, the ad is attracting downloaders not buyers — that's a creative and targeting problem, not a setter problem. Tell whoever runs the ads (section 14).

Play 02 · Ad leads

Who is this? / I didn't message you / What ad?

What it usually means

Genuine confusion. They tapped something on a scroll, or commented a word, and have no memory of it thirty minutes later. Extremely common and completely harmless if you handle it in one message.

Do this

Explain plainly and without defensiveness, reference the specific ad, and give them an easy exit. The exit is what turns confusion into goodwill instead of a spam report.

Re-orient them

Ha, no worries — you commented [keyword] on my post about [topic] earlier, so this auto-sent you [the thing]. Genuinely no obligation, keep it and ignore me if it's not useful 👍

Never

Get defensive, or imply they're lying. Never push into a pitch off the back of confusion — that's exactly the interaction that gets reported, and spam reports raise your costs across the entire ad account.

Play 03 · Ad leads

Is this a bot? Am I talking to a real person?

What it usually means

Your automation ran too long, or your messages read as templated. It's a fair question and it's a test — the answer decides whether they engage at all.

Do this

Break the pattern immediately. Answer as a human, in human language, with something a template couldn't produce — reference the time of day, something specific they said, or offer a voice note.

Prove you're real

Ha, real person — [your name], sat here with a coffee. The first message was automated, everything since has been me. Want me to send a voice note so you can hear I'm not a robot? 😅

Then

If this question comes up often, your handover is too late. Move the human takeover earlier — see section 10.

Never

Claim to be a person while a bot is still running the thread. It always gets caught, and it converts a curious lead into someone who tells other people you're a scam.

Play 04 · Ad leads

Thanks, I've got what I need

What it usually means

Polite close. They took the freebie and they're ending the conversation. Sometimes true, sometimes they just don't know coaching is on the table.

Do this

Accept it gracefully, then make one — one — honest, low-pressure offer of the door. Accepting well is what makes them come back in three months, which a surprising number do.

One open door

No worries at all, hope it helps 👍 Only thing I'd say — if you get three weeks in and it starts slipping, that's usually where people need a hand rather than more information. Door's open if so.

Never

Follow a polite close with three more messages. You paid for the lead, but the lead doesn't owe you a conversation — and pushing here is what generates blocks and reports.

Play 05 · Ad leads

They're outside your service area or country

What it usually means

Targeting leak, or your ad got organic reach beyond its audience. On broad campaigns this is routine.

Do this

Decide fast whether you can genuinely serve them. If your offer is fully online and you can cover their timezone, brilliant — carry on. If not, say so quickly and warmly, and log it: a cluster of out-of-area leads is a targeting fix worth real money.

Out of area

Ah — I'm [your base] based and the way I coach means I need to be in a similar timezone to you, so I'd be doing you a disservice taking you on. Keep the [freebie] though, all of it still applies 👍

Play 06 · Ad leads

The lead arrives at 2am while you're asleep

What it usually means

Your ads are delivering outside your working hours. Normal, and it's the main structural reason paid leads go cold — five-minute response is impossible at 3am.

Do this

Two fixes, and you want both. First: an automation that instantly delivers the promised thing and sets an honest expectation. Second: work overnight leads first thing, before anything else in your day, and open by acknowledging the gap rather than pretending it didn't happen.

Overnight auto-reply

Here's [the thing] 👇 [deliver it] It's [late] here so I'll come back to you properly in the morning — [your name]

Morning pick-up

Morning! Sorry, that came through while I was asleep. Did [the thing] come through alright?

Then

If a large share of leads land outside your hours, either narrow ad scheduling to hours you can cover or add cover. Paying for leads you cannot answer for eight hours is the most avoidable waste in the account.

Play 07 · Ad leads

But the ad said [something you don't actually offer]

What it usually means

A gap between the ad's promise and your real offer. Either the creative overpromised, or they read into it. Either way they are right to raise it.

Do this

Honour whatever the ad actually said, to the letter, even if it costs you. Then be straight about what you do offer. Flag the mismatch to whoever writes the ads the same day — if one person surfaced it, dozens felt it and said nothing.

Own the gap

You're right, and that's on us — that wording isn't clear enough. [Honour exactly what was promised.] What I actually do is [the real offer, plainly]. If that's not what you were after, genuinely no hard feelings.

Never

Argue that the ad technically said something else. You will lose, publicly, in a screenshot — and misleading advertising is a regulatory problem, not just an awkward conversation.

Play 08 · Ad leads

Can you guarantee I'll get [the ad's result]?

What it usually means

The ad created an expectation and they're checking whether it's real. Often the last question before they'd commit — which makes it tempting to fudge. Don't.

Do this

Never guarantee an outcome. Replace the guarantee with two things that are true: what you commit to on your side, and any actual guarantee you genuinely offer (a refund term, a trial period) stated exactly as it is written.

Honest on outcomes

Honestly, no — anyone who guarantees you a specific result is lying to you, because it depends as much on you as on me. What I can guarantee is [what you actually deliver — the plan, the check-ins, the responsiveness], and [your actual refund/trial terms]. That's the bit that's in my control.

Never

Promise a weight, a timeline or a body composition. It's an unlawful claim in many places, it's grounds for a refund everywhere, and it attracts exactly the clients who will be furious when biology doesn't cooperate.

Play 09 · Ad leads

Why do I keep seeing your ads everywhere?

What it usually means

Mild irritation at retargeting, sometimes a genuine privacy question. Rarely hostile.

Do this

Be straightforward and slightly self-aware about it. Defensiveness or pretending not to know how it works both read badly.

Own the retargeting

Ha, sorry — you looked at something of mine at some point so the algorithm decided we're best friends now 😅 You can hide them from the three dots on the ad if they're doing your head in. Either way, here's the [thing], no strings.

Play 10 · Ad leads

The same person comes in twice from two different ads

What it usually means

They're interested enough to click twice. That's a buying signal, not an admin problem — though it does mean you paid twice.

Do this

Merge the conversation, don't restart it. Reference the earlier thread so they don't feel processed, and note the duplicate for your ad reporting — duplicate leads inflate your apparent volume and quietly distort your cost per lead.

Merge, don't restart

Think we've already got one going from the [other ad] one 😄 Picking up where we left off — you said [their thing], right?

Play 11 · Ad leads

They reply days later, after the messaging window has closed

What it usually means

They came back on their own. Their message reopens the window — so this is a live lead again, and a warmer one than when they arrived.

Do this

Respond fast and treat it as a fresh, warm conversation. No guilt, no reference to the delay. Re-ask the timeline question, because their situation has probably changed — that's usually why they're back.

Warm restart

Good to hear from you! Did you get anywhere with [their goal], or is it still sat on the to-do list?

Never

"Oh, you're alive!" Even as a joke. They came back — don't make it awkward.

Play 12 · Ad leads

Obvious spam, a bot, or a scam approach

Signs

Instant generic replies, links to crypto or "growth services", requests to move to another app immediately, an account with no history, messages that don't respond to what you said.

Do this

Don't engage, don't click anything, report and block. Log it as spam so it doesn't pollute your lead numbers — if a specific ad or placement produces a lot of it, that's worth knowing and worth excluding.

Never

Click a link, move to another app, or send anything. And never let a spam lead sit in your pipeline counted as a real one — it makes your cost per lead look better than it is, which leads you to scale the wrong ad.

Play 13 · Gone quiet

Left on read for under an hour

What it usually means

Nothing. They're driving, working, or scrolled past. On paid traffic the gap between messages is usually just life.

Do this

Nothing. Do not double-text inside the hour. Move to the next lead — with paid flow there is always a next lead.

Never

"You still there?" · "??" · "Hello?" — the three most expensive characters in DM setting, and on ad traffic they generate blocks.

Play 14 · Gone quiet

Quiet for 4–12 hours mid-conversation

What it usually means

Forgot, or your last message was too much work. Check it — if it was long, asked two questions, or asked something requiring thought, that's your answer.

Do this

One short bump that's easy to answer and doesn't mention the silence. If your last question was heavy, re-ask it in a lighter form. Remember the clock: on a paid lead this bump is more urgent than it would be organically.

Soft bump

No rush at all — just didn't want this to get buried. Where did you land on [the thing]?

Never

Guilt them, or apologise for messaging ("sorry to bother you!"). Both lower your standing in the conversation.

Play 15 · Gone quiet

They reply with just 👍 or "haha" or "true"

What it usually means

Polite closing. Not rejection — just no reason to keep typing. You have to supply one.

Do this

One open, low-cost, slightly personal question. Never a statement — a statement lets them thumbs-up you again.

Re-open

Ha — genuinely though, how's training going at the minute? Good patch or a messy one?

Play 16 · Gone quiet

Three one-word answers in a row

What it usually means

Wrong lead or wrong moment. You're pulling the conversation uphill. With paid volume, continuing costs you the leads you're not talking to.

Do this

One calibrated question that lets them off the hook honestly, then park based on the answer.

The honest out

Can I ask you straight — is this something you actually want to sort right now, or have I caught you at a bad time? Either's completely fine.

Play 17 · Gone quiet

They block you, or report the ad

What it usually means

A no, delivered without words. Occasionally it means your approach in that thread was too pushy — worth an honest look.

Do this

Stop. Log it. Watch the rate: isolated blocks are noise, but a rising block-and-report rate degrades ad delivery and raises costs across the account. If it's climbing, the problem is upstream — the ad's promise, the speed of the pitch, or an over-long bot sequence.

Never

Message from a second account or through a mutual. Fastest route to a screenshot and to losing the ad account entirely.

Play 18 · Money

How much is it?

What it usually means

Asked early — which on ad traffic is most of the time — it's a filtering question, not a buying signal. They want to know whether to keep reading. It's completely reasonable, and paid leads ask it sooner and blunter than organic ones because they have no relationship to lose.

Do this

Give a real range, then earn two questions. Dodging entirely reads as "it's expensive and I'm hiding it" — worse than any number.

Early price question

Fair question — it runs from [£X] to [£Y] a month depending on how much support you need. I'd rather not throw a number at you before I know if I can even help though. Can I ask two quick things first?

Never

Answer "it depends" and stop. Answer only with "let's get on a call". Both feel evasive, and an ad lead who feels handled simply leaves — they have no reason not to.

Play 19 · Money

No seriously, just tell me the price

What it usually means

They asked twice. They've decided you're being slippery, and they're right. Trust is draining in real time.

Do this

Answer straight, immediately, with a number. Then offer the context as a separate, optional thing.

Straight answer

Course — [£X] a month, minimum [3] months. That's [what's included]. Want me to break down what that looks like week to week?

Rule

Never dodge price twice. One deferral with a range is professional. Two is evasive — and on a lead who cost you money, evasive is expensive.

Play 20 · Money

That's too expensive

What it usually means

Two completely different things, and you can't respond well until you know which. Either the money genuinely isn't there, or they're not convinced it's worth it yet. The first is a budget fact; the second is a value conversation. On paid traffic the first is more common than organic, because ads reach people who never chose to be in your world.

Do this

Separate them with one question, asked without a hint of defensiveness.

The split

Totally fair. Just so I know which it is — is it more that the number's out of reach at the minute, or that you're not convinced it's worth it yet? Genuinely no wrong answer.

If it's out of reach

Be honest and kind. Offer the lower tier only if you genuinely run one. Otherwise: "Honestly then, now's not the time and that's completely fine. Can I check back in [month]? Here's [useful free thing] in the meantime." Then log it as a price-fit issue — a run of these means your ads are reaching the wrong income bracket.

If it's a value question

Belongs on the call. "Fair thing to want to test. That's exactly what the call's for — I'll walk you through what month one actually looks like and you decide."

Never

Suggest credit cards, buy-now-pay-later, or borrowing. Never use "you're worth the investment" as pressure. Pushing someone into a payment they can't afford produces a refund, a chargeback and a public review — and on paid traffic those reviews sit right under your ads.

Play 21 · Money

Can you do me a discount?

What it usually means

Often reflex rather than a real barrier. Sometimes play 20 in a different hat.

Do this

Hold the price, offer less scope instead. Discounting the same service teaches everyone the price was never real — including current clients, who talk to each other.

Hold the line, warmly

I keep the price the same for everyone — wouldn't be fair on the people already paying it. What I can do is [smaller option] at [£Y]. Want me to explain how that one works?

Play 22 · Money

Can I pay in instalments / start next month?

What it usually means

Usually genuine cash-flow timing, not a value objection. Often very close to a yes.

Do this

Offer only what you actually operate. If you have a split option, name it with the real total. If not, offer a start date on their payday — and honour it.

Payday start

I don't run instalments, but no reason we can't start [date after payday]. I'll hold you a spot and send everything the day before. Work for you?

Never

Invent a payment plan your systems can't take, or quote a split that hides the total. Always state the full amount.

Play 23 · Timing

I'll have a think about it

What it usually means

One specific unresolved thing they don't want to say out loud — nearly always money, time, or "will this work for someone like me". Your job is to find which, not to overcome it.

Do this

Give three named doors. People pick a door far more readily than they volunteer an objection.

Three doors

Course — what's the bit you want to think over? The money, the time it takes, or whether it'll actually work for you? Whichever it is I'd rather just talk about it straight.

Then

Address that one thing, then agree a specific day to speak. "Let me know!" is where paid leads die — replace it with "Shall I check in Thursday?", and get an email while you're at it, because Thursday may be outside the window.

Play 24 · Timing

I'm too busy at the minute

What it usually means

Either genuinely swamped this fortnight, or "not a priority" said politely. Different responses — don't assume.

Do this

Acknowledge honestly, then separate short-term chaos from a seasonal no.

Busy, split

Makes sense — and honestly being busy is why most people bring me in, the whole point is that it fits round everything else. But genuinely: is this a "not this fortnight" or a "not this year"? I'd rather know so I stop bugging you.

Never

Argue with their schedule or imply they'd find time if they cared. Telling someone their priorities are wrong has never once produced a sale.

Play 25 · Timing

Ask me after my holiday / in January

What it usually means

Sometimes a real date, sometimes a soft no. Treating it as real costs nothing and wins the ones who meant it.

Do this

Pin the exact date, say you'll message, diarise it, and actually do it. Get an email — on paid traffic a six-week gap guarantees the DM window is shut.

Pin the date

No problem. When are you back — [date]? I'll put a note in my diary for the week after. What's your email so it doesn't get lost in here? Enjoy it 👍

The promised follow-up

Hey [name] — said I'd message when you were back. How was [the holiday]? Still fancy getting stuck into [goal]?

Play 26 · Timing

I need to speak to my partner first

What it usually means

Usually genuine — shared expense, shared time. Occasionally a polite exit. Respect it either way; a partner who feels bypassed vetoes on principle.

Do this

Support the conversation rather than route around it. Arm them with the three things their partner will ask: cost, time, and what changes at home.

Arm them

Completely get it. So you've got the actual numbers: [£X] a month, about [X] hours a week, and the main change at home is [the realistic one]. Happy to jump on a quick call with both of you if easier. When are you speaking to them?

Never

"It's your body, it's your decision." Anything that nudges them around their partner is manipulative and loses the deal roughly every time.

Play 27 · Trust

I've had a coach before and it didn't work

What it usually means

Gold, not an objection. They've already proven they'll pay for coaching — which on cold paid traffic is the hardest thing to establish.

Do this

Get curious and let them talk. Best qualifying question you'll ever ask.

Dig in

That's really useful to know — what went wrong? Was it the plan itself, or more that you didn't get the support you needed around it?

Then

Name specifically how you work differently on that exact thing. If you don't actually work differently, say so — you lose one sale and gain a reputation.

Never

Slag off the other coach. Reads as insecure, and the client often still likes them.

Play 28 · Trust

Have you worked with anyone like me?

What it usually means

"I'm worried I'm a special case." Usually about age, body type, shift work, an injury, or having a lot to lose.

Do this

One specific, relevant, true example with the detail that makes it land. One real story beats ten vague claims.

One real example

Yeah — [first name or "a client"], [similar situation]. [What changed and roughly how long it took.] Everyone's different, but the pattern's usually the same.

Never

Invent a client. Share a photo, name or result without written permission. Use someone else's transformation images. All three are sackable, potentially legal, and on a paid account they're also an ad-policy violation waiting to happen.

If the honest answer is no

"Not with that exact thing, no. What I'd do is [specific plan], and if I thought you needed someone more specialist I'd tell you."

Play 29 · Trust

How do I know this isn't a scam?

What it usually means

Reasonable caution, and more common on paid traffic than organic — they met you through an advert, which is exactly how scams arrive too. Don't take it personally; defensiveness is what they're testing for.

Do this

Be boringly transparent. Real name, real business, real terms, nothing paid before a conversation.

Transparency

Completely fair thing to ask — you met me through an ad, you should be checking. I'm [full name], I coach under [business] — [website]. Nothing gets paid before we've spoken, you get terms in writing, and [refund policy in one line]. Have a look and ask me anything.

Play 30 · Trust

They keep asking for free advice and never engage with anything else

What it usually means

Either they don't know coaching is an option, or they've decided your inbox is a free service. Test the first before you conclude the second.

Do this

One genuinely good answer, then transition once, cleanly. If they take it and ask another, cap it warmly.

Answer, then transition

[Genuinely useful answer, 2–3 lines.] That'll get you moving. Out of interest — are you piecing this together yourself, or would you rather someone just built it round you?

The kind cap

Happy to answer the odd thing, but this is properly the stuff I get paid for so I can't build you a plan over DM 😅 If you ever want it done properly, say the word.

Play 31 · Logistics

Do you do in-person? I'd prefer face to face

What it usually means

Rarely about location at heart — it's worry about accountability and technique.

Do this

Answer the factual question, then speak to the concern underneath. Never oversell online as identical to in-person; name what it does better and what it doesn't.

Online, honestly

[Straight answer on in-person.] What most people are actually worried about is form and accountability — I handle that with [video form checks / weekly calls / daily check-ins]. What online does better is you get me every week rather than one hour. What it can't do is physically move your elbow, so I'm fussy about video.

Play 32 · Logistics

Can you just send me a programme?

What it usually means

They believe the missing piece is information. Based on what they've told you it usually isn't — but "you don't know what you need" is not the move.

Do this

Sell what you actually sell. If you have a programme-only product, offer it honestly. If not, explain the gap without being sniffy.

Programme vs coaching

I can — but going off what you said, the plan isn't the bit that's broken. You said you're fine for three weeks then one bad week knocks you off. A PDF doesn't fix that, a person does. ["Happy to sell you the plan on its own at £X if you'd rather start there."]

Play 33 · Logistics

I'm in a completely different timezone

What it usually means

Practical question about whether you'll actually be available. Common on broad paid targeting — see also play 05.

Do this

Confirm only what you can genuinely commit to. Offer times in their local time and always write the timezone out.

Timezone booking

No problem — whereabouts are you? I'll send times in your local time. Day to day, check-ins are [async/voice note] so the gap doesn't matter; calls I'd do [your realistic window].

Play 34 · Logistics

They ask something you genuinely don't know

What it usually means

Nothing bad. It happens constantly and is not a test.

Do this

Say you don't know, say when you'll come back, then come back. Bluffing loses far more deals than admitting a gap — and a confident wrong answer about training or nutrition can genuinely hurt someone.

Honest gap

Honest answer — not sure off the top of my head and I don't want to guess at something that matters. Let me check properly and come back to you tonight.

Play 35 · Sensitive

They turn out to be under 18

Why it matters

Safeguarding, insurance and in many places law. Ad targeting has age settings but they leak — minors reach paid fitness content constantly, so expect this and know the answer in advance.

Do this

Stop the sales conversation. Don't coach a minor without a parent or guardian involved and consenting, and follow your insurer's and your jurisdiction's requirements. Be warm — they're a kid who wants to get fitter, which is a good thing.

Under 18

Ah — I can't take anyone under 18 on without a parent or guardian involved, that's just how it has to work. Genuinely though, fair play for taking it seriously. If a parent wants to chat, they're very welcome to message me.

Never

Continue a one-to-one sales conversation with a minor, take payment from a minor, or discuss weight loss, calorie targets or body composition with them. Also check your ad's age targeting the same day.

Play 36 · Sensitive

You see possible signs of disordered eating

Signs to watch for

Requests for extreme deficits or very rapid loss. Mentions of purging, laxatives, multi-day fasting, or "earning" food. Intense body-hate language. Already very lean and wanting to lose more. Fixation on a number that doesn't match what they describe.

Do this

Leave sales mode entirely and do not return to it in this conversation. Respond as a person, don't diagnose, don't lecture, signpost to appropriate support. Right human decision and right business decision are identical here: do not take this sale.

Step out of sales

I'm going to be straight with you because I'd rather be honest than get a client. What you're describing isn't something I'd coach — taking it on would risk making things worse for you, and I'm not the right person for it. Talking to your GP or a specialist about it would be a genuinely good move. There's real support out there and it does help. I'm not disappearing on you, I'm just not the right fit for this.

Never

Sell them a fat-loss package. Give a calorie target. Say they "just need discipline". Diagnose anything. Flag it to your lead coach — and if a particular ad angle keeps surfacing this, that creative needs rethinking, not just this conversation.

Play 37 · Sensitive

They disclose an injury, medical condition, medication or pregnancy

Why it matters

Scope of practice. A training or nutrition qualification doesn't make you a physiotherapist, doctor or registered dietitian, and acting like one is how coaches get sued and clients get hurt.

Do this

Thank them, don't speculate, be clear where your lane ends. Then either work alongside their clinician with clearance, or refer out.

Scope of practice

Thanks for flagging that — good that you did. I'm not going to guess at anything medical, that's not my lane. What I'd normally do is work around it with clearance from [your GP / physio / consultant], and stay in touch with them if they're happy to. Have you spoken to them about training?

Never

"That's fine, we'll train around it" without clearance. Comment on their medication. Suggest changing a prescription. Claim training will treat, cure or manage a diagnosed condition — in an ad or in a DM.

Play 38 · Sensitive

The conversation turns flirty or inappropriate

What it usually means

Doesn't matter — the response is the same regardless of intent. Expect this more often on paid traffic, where strangers arrive with no social context at all.

Do this

One clear, unapologetic boundary. No joke to soften it, no laughing emoji — ambiguity gets read as an invitation. If it continues at all: screenshot, block, report, tell your lead coach.

The boundary

I'm going to keep this professional — that's not what this is. If you want to talk training I'm happy to, otherwise I'll leave it here.

Never

Play along to keep a sale alive. Reply "haha 😂" and change the subject. Keep going out of politeness. No lead is worth this, whatever it cost.

Play 39 · Sensitive

They're rude, sarcastic or aggressive

What it usually means

Usually about their day, not you — and on ad traffic, sometimes just irritation at being advertised to. Occasionally someone fishing for a reaction to screenshot.

Do this

Never match it. One neutral, gracious line, then leave. Write every message assuming it'll be screenshotted, because the hostile ones usually are — and yours is a business with its name on an advert.

Exit gracefully

All good — I'll leave you to it. If you ever do want a hand with it, you know where I am 👍

Play 40 · Sensitive

They disclose something heavy — grief, a breakup, mental health

What it usually means

They're telling you the real reason training fell apart. It's trust, and it deserves to be treated as trust rather than as an opening.

Do this

Be a person. Acknowledge it properly and make no offer in that message — not even a soft one. If it's right to pick it back up, do it days later and let them lead.

Human first

Ah mate, I'm really sorry. That's a lot to be carrying and training is rightly the last thing on the list right now. No agenda from me at all — if you fancy picking it up down the line, just shout.

Never

Use it as leverage ("training would really help with that right now"). Even when true, saying it in that moment is transparently self-serving and people remember it.

Play 41 · After the call

They no-show the booked call

What it usually means

About half are genuine — work overran, kid kicked off, phone on silent. The rest got cold feet. Paid leads no-show more than organic ones, because the relationship is thinner. Assume the best out loud, plan for the rest.

Do this

Message within ten minutes, assume the best, offer one reschedule with two specific times. Send it by text as well as DM — the window may have closed.

No-show · +10 min

No worries if something's come up — I know how it goes. Want to grab [tomorrow 6:40pm] or [Thursday 1:10pm] instead?

If they no-show twice

Stop booking. Move them to the ladder in section 09 and log it. A rising no-show rate on a specific ad usually means that ad's promise is attracting curiosity rather than intent.

Play 42 · After the call

They said yes, then went silent on payment

What it usually means

Usually friction or second thoughts, not dishonesty. The link didn't work, the card declined, they wanted to check the account, or the excitement faded on the drive home.

Do this

Remove friction before you apply any pressure. Two messages maximum, then let it breathe.

Message 1 · same day

Hey [name] — did the link come through alright? Shout if anything's playing up and I'll sort it another way.

Message 2 · +48h

No stress either way — has anything changed since we spoke? Completely fine if you've had second thoughts, I'd just rather know than keep the spot held.

Never

Invent a deadline or claim the price is going up when it isn't. Fake urgency is the most common reason people feel scammed by fitness coaches — and when you're running ads, that feeling ends up in public comments under them.

09

The follow-up ladder

For a qualified lead who's gone quiet. Compressed hard compared with an organic ladder, because the messaging window and the decay curve both work against you. Everything that can happen on day one should happen on day one.

Window

Rungs 1–3 are designed to land inside a 24-hour window. Everything after depends on either a reply from them (which reopens it), a channel you captured, or whatever your platform permits — check section 02 before you automate any of this.

Never send two rungs in the same day, and never send one immediately after an unanswered one. If they reply at any point you leave the ladder and go back to the conversation.

+2–4 hours · same day

The easy bump

Light, and re-asks your question in a form that takes five seconds. Tighter than you'd use organically — you're racing the clock.

Quick one so I don't leave you hanging — was it more [option A] or [option B]?
+8–12 hours · still day one

Value, with no ask attached

One genuinely useful thing for the exact problem they described. No question, no pitch. Resets you from "someone selling me" to "someone helping me", and it's your best shot at a reply that reopens the window.

Not chasing you — this just made me think of what you said about [their problem]: [tip / short voice note]. Might be useful.
Day 2 · the last in-window touch

The direct one

Light but unambiguous, and it explicitly offers the channel swap. This is your last reliable chance to get an email before the window shuts.

Did you want to park this or pick it back up? Either's fine — and if DMs are a nightmare for you, give me your email and I'll send it there instead.
Day 4 · window-dependent

The reframe

A new angle on the problem, or a relevant (real, permitted) client outcome that maps onto their situation. Send by email if you captured one — deliverability is better and you're not fighting a policy.

Random — just had a client hit [specific thing] and she started almost exactly where you described. Made me think of you. How's it going?
Day 7 · window-dependent

The permission close

The most effective message in the ladder. Asking permission to stop gets replies, because it's easy to answer and disarmingly honest. Mean it.

Totally fine if the timing's off — want me to stop bringing it up? Genuinely no hard feelings either way.
Day 14+ · owned channels only

Hand them to email. Stop DMing.

Move them out of active DM follow-up and into your email nurture, if they consented to it. No more direct messages unless they message you — at this distance you're almost certainly outside the window anyway, and continuing is what generates the reports that raise your ad costs.

Day 60–90 · retargeting

Let the ads do the follow-up

This is the real advantage of paid: a lead who went cold in the DMs can be re-reached by a retargeting audience without you messaging them at all. Add unconverted leads to a custom audience where your platform and local data rules allow it, and let a different creative do the asking.

10

Bot vs human: where the handover goes

Most paid DM setups run some automation. The question is never whether to use it but exactly where it stops — and almost everyone leaves it running too long.

Let the bot do
  • Instant acknowledgement, day or night
  • Delivering the exact thing the ad promised
  • Capturing the keyword and tagging the ad source
  • One qualifying question, maximum
  • Telling them a human is coming and roughly when
Never let the bot do
  • Qualifying past one question
  • Anything involving price
  • The transition or the booking ask
  • Objection handling of any kind
  • Pretending to be a named person

The handover rule

A human takes over on the lead's first real reply. Not their third, not after a qualifying sequence — the first time they type an actual sentence, a person is answering. Every extra automated message after that point increases the chance of play 03 ("is this a bot?"), and a lead who decides they're talking to software stops telling you anything true.

Two practical settings that matter more than the scripts: make sure the bot stops the moment a human replies (nothing is worse than an automation talking over you mid-conversation), and make sure the ad source, creative and keyword are written onto the conversation record automatically. You cannot do section 14 without that tagging, and adding it retroactively is miserable.

Never

Give a bot a human name and let it run a conversation. When it's discovered — and it is discovered — you haven't just lost that lead, you've handed them a story about your business that they will tell other people.

The daily rhythm

Built around ad delivery rather than around your preferences. Leads arrive when the ads spend, so the working day is structured in response windows, not tasks. Tick these off as you go — they save in this browser.

Watch

The most common failure mode in paid DM is batching. Checking the inbox three times a day feels efficient and quietly destroys the economics — a lead answered at 4pm that arrived at 9am converts a fraction as well as one answered at 9:05. If you cannot cover the hours your ads run, narrow the ad schedule rather than accept the decay.

Unit economics

The difference between organic and paid setting is that here, every ratio has a currency attached. Know these four numbers for your own account and you can tell instantly whether a bad week is a traffic problem or a setting problem.

CPL Cost per lead in the inbox ad spend ÷ leads
CPQL Cost per qualified lead spend ÷ qualified
CPBC Cost per booked call spend ÷ bookings
CAC Cost to acquire a client spend ÷ clients

The number that governs whether you can scale is CAC against the lifetime value of a client. If a client is worth £900 over their average stay and costs £300 to acquire, you can spend more and grow. If they're worth £400 and cost £380, you have a business that gets more fragile the more you spend on it — and no DM script fixes that.

Feeding the ad account

The part that doesn't exist in organic setting, and the part that compounds. You are the only person in the business who talks to the traffic. What you notice in the inbox is worth more to the ad account than any dashboard.

Tag every lead at the source

If nothing else in this section happens, make this happen. Every conversation should carry the ad, the creative and the keyword it came from, written on automatically at the point of arrival. Without it you're averaging good ads and bad ads together, which means you can't scale either.

Log why every dead lead died

One field, a short list of reasons, filled in every time. These aren't lost deals, they're a targeting brief:

The weekly handover

Whoever runs the ads — you, a media buyer, an agency — gets this every week, in five lines. It takes ten minutes and it's the highest-leverage ten minutes in the operation.

  1. Best ad this week — by cost per booked call, not by cost per lead. The cheapest leads are frequently the worst ones.
  2. Worst ad this week — and the reason from your dead-lead log, not a guess.
  3. The phrase they keep using — the exact words leads use to describe their problem. This is free ad copy and it beats anything written from imagination.
  4. The question three or more people asked — an objection appearing repeatedly in DMs belongs in the ad creative, where it gets handled before the click.
  5. Anything that felt off — a spike in "is this a bot", a rise in blocks, confusion about what was promised. You'll feel these a week before they show up in the numbers.
The loop

Better ads produce better leads. Better leads make the setter look brilliant and drop CAC. Lower CAC means more spend is affordable, which means more leads. This loop is the actual business — the scripts just determine how fast it spins.

Rewrite every script in your own voice first; a script you can't say out loud naturally will sound exactly like a script, and on paid traffic people are already braced for one.
