Coach enablement · v1

Every DM has a next right move.

This is the whole system: how to open, how to qualify, how to get from a story reply to a booked call, and — the part most coaches get wrong — exactly what to say when something unexpected happens. Read it once end to end. After that, live in section 07.

5 pipeline stages 32 situation plays 7 follow-up touches Read time ~25 min
Core

The DM does not sell coaching. The DM books the call. That is its entire job. Every message you send should make the next reply easier to give, and move the conversation one step closer to a specific time in a calendar. If you find yourself pasting a price list, a programme outline, or a 200-word pitch into a DM, you have already lost the thread — get back to booking the call.

01

The seven rules

Break these and no script below will save you. These are non-negotiable across every conversation, every lead source, every coach on the team.

RULE 01 One question per message. A DM is a conversation, not a form. Two questions in one message means they answer the easy one and ignore the important one. Ask one thing, wait, then ask the next.
RULE 02 Match their energy and their length. They send six words, you send six to fifteen. They use one emoji, you use at most one. If they write in lowercase with no punctuation, don't reply with a formatted essay. Mirroring builds rapport faster than anything you can say.
RULE 03 Never pitch in the first message. Not a soft pitch. Not "let me know if you ever want help with that." The first message earns a reply and nothing else. Pitching on message one is the single fastest way to train someone to ignore you forever.
RULE 04 Get permission before you offer. "Can I ask you something?" — "Do you want my honest take?" — "Want me to have a look at it?" A yes before the offer changes the whole frame: now they're pulling, not being pushed.
RULE 05 Never say anything you can't back up. No guaranteed results. No "you'll lose 10kg in 6 weeks." No medical or clinical claims. No testimonials or before/afters you don't have written permission to use. One exaggeration that gets screenshotted costs more than a year of closed deals.
RULE 06 Two unanswered messages, then stop. Never send a third message into silence in the same window. Back off, use the follow-up ladder in section 08, and let time do the work. Chasing reads as desperate and desperate doesn't close.
RULE 07 A clear "no" ends it, permanently. When someone says no, thank them, leave the door open once, and never bring it up again unless they do. Respecting a no is what makes the yeses arrive months later — and roughly a third of them do.
02

The five stages

Every conversation that ends in a client passes through these five stages in this order. They are numbered because the order genuinely matters — you cannot qualify someone who hasn't replied, and you cannot book someone you haven't qualified. Before you send any message, know which stage you're in and what it needs to achieve.

1

Open

Goal: a real, human reply. Not a like, not an emoji — words.

Messages: 1–2 Time: minutes Exit when: they write a sentence

The opener is about them and costs them almost nothing to answer. It refers to something specific and real — their story, their comment, their post, the thing they actually asked for. Generic openers ("Hey! How's training going? 💪") get generic outcomes.

If you cannot name a specific reason you are messaging this person, do not message this person.

Do
  • Reference the exact thing they posted or said
  • Ask something answerable in under ten words
  • Use their first name once, spelled right
  • Send one message, then wait
Don't
  • Open with "Hey hun" or "Hey beautiful"
  • Send a paragraph as your first message
  • Compliment something you didn't actually look at
  • Copy-paste the identical opener to 200 people
2

Qualify

Goal: understand the goal, the history, the real obstacle and the timeline.

Messages: 4–8 Time: same day, sometimes 2–3 days Exit when: you can state their problem back to them

This is where the deal is actually made, and it's the stage most coaches rush. You are trying to learn four things — covered properly in section 04 — and, just as importantly, to make them feel heard. Most people have never had anyone ask them careful questions about their training and it lands harder than any pitch.

Test for exiting this stage: can you write one sentence that describes their situation so accurately they'd say "yeah, exactly"? If not, ask another question.

3

Transition

Goal: permission to talk about working together.

Messages: 1–2 Time: immediately after qualifying Exit when: they say yes to hearing more

The bridge from friendly chat to business. It has two beats: reflect their problem back, then ask permission. Skipping the permission beat is what makes a conversation feel like it suddenly turned into an advert. Full scripts in section 05.

4

Book

Goal: a specific time on a specific day, in the calendar, with the right contact details.

Messages: 2–3 Time: aim for a slot inside 72 hours Exit when: invite sent and they've confirmed receipt

"When are you free?" is not booking — it's homework. Offer two concrete times. Sooner is better: a call booked nine days out shows up far less often than one booked for Thursday.

5

Confirm & show

Goal: they actually turn up. This stage exists because booked ≠ showed.

Touches: 3 When: on booking · night before · 1 hour before Exit when: the call happens

Most coaches stop at "booked" and then wonder why half their calendar evaporates. Three short, warm touches routinely take show rates from around half to the high seventies. Exact messages in section 06.

03

Openers by lead source

The same opener does not work everywhere. What you say depends entirely on how they arrived and how warm they are. Warm sources first — always work your warm inbox to zero before you start any cold outreach.

A. They replied to your story

The warmest lead you will get. They raised their hand. Do not waste it by replying with a thumbs up.

Story: "most people train hard and recover like idiots"
ha this is me 😅
Ha — what's your version of it? Sleep, food, or just never taking a deload?
One question. Gives them three easy doors in.
honestly all three but mostly sleep, i'm up at 5 for work
That's a rough hand to play. How long have you been training around the 5am starts?
Now you're in Stage 2 and it took two messages.

B. They just followed you

New follower · opener

Hey [name] — thanks for the follow. Curious what brought you over, are you training at the minute or trying to get back into it?

The binary question ("training / getting back into it") does the heavy lifting. Either answer tells you something, and both are easy to send back one-handed.

C. They commented on a post

Commenter · opener

Hey [name], saw your comment on the [topic] post — you said [quote their words back]. Is that something you're dealing with right now or more of a past thing?

D. They downloaded your free guide / lead magnet

Message within the hour while the intent is hot. Deliver value first, ask second.

Lead magnet · opener

Hey [name] — sent the [guide name] over, let me know if it didn't land. Quick one while you're here: what made you grab it? Trying to sort a specific thing or just having a look?

E. Old lead you spoke to before

No guilt, no "you went quiet on me". Treat it as a clean slate.

Re-engagement · 60–90 days later

Hey [name], been a while — no agenda, just thought of you. Last time we spoke you were trying to sort [their exact goal]. Did you end up cracking it?

F. Cold outreach

The lowest-yield source and the easiest to do badly. Only message people with a genuine, visible reason — they follow you, they're in a community you're in, they posted publicly about the thing you help with. Twenty researched messages will beat two hundred blasted ones, and the blasted ones will get your account restricted.

Cold · opener

Hey [name] — random one, I saw your post in [group/place] about [specific thing]. I coach people through exactly that. Not pitching you, genuinely just curious — did you get it sorted?

Careful

"Not pitching you" is only usable if it is true in that message. If they reply and you immediately pitch, you've lied and they'll feel it. Earn the conversation first.

04

Qualifying: the GAPT questions

Four things you need to know before you offer anything. Ask them in this order, one message at a time, and react to each answer like a human before moving on. If you fire all four back-to-back it reads like an interrogation and people bail.

G — GOAL What are they actually trying to do? "What are you working towards at the minute?" · "If this went perfectly, what's different in six months?" — You want something concrete. "Get in shape" is not a goal, it's a mood. Push gently for specifics: a number, an event, a feeling they can describe.
A — ATTEMPTS What have they already tried? "What have you tried so far?" · "What's worked best of everything you've done?" — This tells you whether they're a beginner or someone who's been burned, and it's the single best predictor of how you should pitch on the call.
P — PAIN Where does it actually break down? "What's the bit that keeps tripping you up?" · "Where does it usually fall apart — the training or the food?" — People rarely lack information. They lack structure, accountability or a plan that survives a bad week. Find the specific breakdown point.
T — TIMELINE Is this a now thing or a someday thing? "Is this a 'starting now' thing or more of a 'thinking about it' thing?" — The most skipped question and the most useful. It's a permission-giving question: it lets them tell you they're not ready without feeling awkward, and it saves you a week of chasing someone who was never going to start until March.

Reading the answers

Once you have all four, score the conversation. Be honest with yourself here — coaches lose entire weeks pushing amber leads that should have been parked.

Green — go now

Specific goal. Specific-ish deadline or trigger event. Has tried on their own and it hasn't worked. Describes the pain in their own words without you dragging it out. Asks you a question back about how you work.

Action: transition immediately. Do not add more value, do not send another tip. Move to section 05 in your very next message.

Amber — nurture

Vague goal, no timeline, engaged but casual. Interested in the topic rather than in changing anything. Often genuinely lovely to talk to, which is exactly why they eat your time.

Action: give one genuinely useful thing, make no offer, and set a diary reminder to check in at a natural moment — after their holiday, start of the month, after the event they mentioned. Then leave them alone until then.

Red — exit gracefully

Not looking for coaching. Only wants free programming. Cannot afford it under any structure you actually offer. Needs a doctor, physio or registered dietitian rather than a coach. Under 18 without a guardian involved. Showing signs of disordered eating (see play 25).

Action: give one useful thing for free, say clearly and kindly that you're not the right fit, and end it warmly. Red leads who are treated well send referrals. Red leads who are pushed leave reviews.

05

The transition

Two beats, in this order, and never skip beat one. This is the highest-leverage twenty seconds in the whole conversation.

  1. Reflect. Say their problem back to them in your own words, accurately and without embellishment. You are proving you listened. If they reply "yeah exactly" or "100%", you've nailed it.
  2. Ask permission. A small, easy yes that opens the door to the offer. Never offer without it.
The transition, in full
yeah I'll be good for 3 weeks then one bad week and I've completely fallen off again
So it's not that you don't know what to do — it's that nothing's holding it together when life gets in the way. Does that sound about right?
Beat 1: reflect.
yes that's exactly it
That's honestly the main thing I fix for people. Can I ask — are you open to having someone coach you through it, or are you set on figuring it out solo?
Beat 2: permission. The "or solo" option is real — let them take it.
no I'm definitely open to it, been thinking about it for a while
Cool. Easiest thing is a quick 15 min call so I can get the full picture and tell you straight whether I can actually help. No pitch, and if I'm not the right fit I'll say so. Want me to send a couple of times?
Transition · beat 1 · reflect

So if I've got this right — it's not that you [the thing they clearly can do], it's that [the actual breakdown point]. Is that fair?

Transition · beat 2 · permission

That's pretty much exactly what I help people with. Can I ask — are you open to having someone coach you through it, or are you set on doing it solo?

Transition · the ask

Easiest thing is a quick 15 minute call so I can hear the full picture and tell you straight whether I can help. No pitch, and if I'm not the right fit I'll tell you. Want me to send over a couple of times?

Why

"If I'm not the right fit I'll tell you" is the most important line in the script — and it only works if you actually do it. Say it, mean it, and turn people away on calls when it's true. The coaches who do this close a higher percentage than the ones who don't, because prospects can feel the difference between someone assessing them and someone hunting them.

06

Booking and getting them to show

Booking is a mechanical skill. Follow the sequence exactly and your show rate takes care of itself.

Offer two times, not a link and not an open question

A booking link is a hurdle, an open question is homework. Two specific times is a decision someone can make in three seconds. Make the times oddly specific — 7:10, 12:40 — because it signals a real calendar rather than a sales grid.

Booking · the two-option close

I've got [Thursday 7:10pm] or [Friday 12:40pm] — which suits you better?

If neither works: "No worries — morning or evening generally better for you?" then offer two more. Never open it up to "whenever you like".

Lock the details, then send the invite

Booking · confirm details

Perfect. What's the best number to reach you on, and an email for the invite?

The three show-up touches

Send all three, every time, to every booking. This is the cheapest lift in the entire system.

Touch 1 · immediately on booking

Invite + expectations + one prep question

Tells them it's real, tells them it's short, and gets a small commitment that makes skipping it feel rude.

Sent the invite over 👍 It'll take about 15 minutes — I'll ask you a bit about your training and where it's getting stuck, then tell you honestly whether I can help.

One thing before we talk: what would need to come out of the call for it to be worth your time?
Touch 2 · night before

The soft confirm

Short. One question. Gives them a graceful way to move it rather than ghost it.

Hey [name] — still good for 7:10 tomorrow?
Touch 3 · one hour before

The practical reminder

Removes every small point of friction: who's calling, on what, and whether they can talk.

Calling you in an hour on [number]. Somewhere you can talk properly?
Note

If they don't reply to touch 2 or touch 3, still make the call at the booked time. A surprising number of people simply don't check DMs and will pick up. Not answering the phone is a no-show — handle it with play 30.

Play 01 · Gone quiet

They've left you on read for less than 24 hours

What it usually means

Nothing. They're at work, driving, with their kids, or they opened it on a lock screen and forgot. Silence under a day carries almost no information.

Do this

Nothing at all. Do not double-text inside the same day. Log it, set the follow-up for tomorrow, move to the next conversation.

Never

"You still there?" · "??" · "Hello?" — these are the three most expensive characters in DM selling.

Play 02 · Gone quiet

They've gone quiet for 24–48 hours mid-conversation

What it usually means

Genuinely forgot, or your last message was too much work to answer. Check your last message — if it was long, or asked two questions, or asked something they'd need to think about, that's your answer.

Do this

One short bump that is easy to answer and doesn't reference the silence. Re-ask in a lighter form if your previous question was heavy.

Soft bump

Hey [name] — no rush at all, just didn't want this to get buried. Where did you land on [the thing]?

Never

Guilt them for not replying, or apologise for messaging ("sorry to bother you!"). Both lower your status in the conversation.

Play 03 · Gone quiet

They reply with just 👍 or "haha" or "true"

What it usually means

They're being polite and closing the conversation gently. It is not rejection — it's a lack of a reason to keep typing. You have to supply one.

Do this

Ask one open, low-cost, slightly personal question. Do not react to the emoji and do not send a statement — a statement lets them thumbs-up you again.

Re-open

Ha — genuinely though, how's training actually going at the minute? Good patch or a messy one?

Play 04 · Gone quiet

Three one-word answers in a row

What it usually means

Wrong person or wrong moment. You are pulling a conversation uphill and they are not helping. Continuing costs you time and costs them goodwill.

Do this

One calibrated question that lets them off the hook honestly, then park it based on the answer.

The honest out

Can I ask you straight — is this something you actually want to sort right now, or have I caught you at a bad time? Either's completely fine.

Then

If they say bad time: agree a specific date to revisit and diarise it. If they say not really: thank them, leave one door open, move on.

Play 05 · Gone quiet

They resurface weeks later out of nowhere

What it usually means

Something changed — a bad weigh-in, a photo they didn't like, a holiday booked, a birthday. They're back because the pain moved, not because they owe you anything.

Do this

Treat it as a brand new conversation with a head start. Pick up warmly, do not mention the gap, and re-run the T of GAPT because their timeline has almost certainly changed.

Warm restart

Good to hear from you! Has anything changed since we last spoke, or is it still [their old goal]?

Never

"Oh look who it is 👀" or "thought you'd disappeared on me". Even jokingly. It makes them feel caught out and they'll go quiet again.

Play 06 · Gone quiet

They unfollow, mute, or block you

What it usually means

A clear no, delivered without words.

Do this

Stop. Mark the lead closed-lost, note the source so you can learn from it, and move on.

Never

Message them from a second account, from your personal account, or through a mutual friend. It is the fastest route to a screenshot in a group chat and, on most platforms, to your account being restricted.

Play 07 · Money

How much is it?

What it usually means

Asked early, this is almost never a buying signal — it's a filtering question. They want to know whether to keep reading. It is also completely reasonable.

Do this

Give a real range, then earn the right to ask two questions. The range matters: dodging entirely reads as "it's expensive and I'm hiding it", which is worse than any number.

Early price question

Fair question — it runs from [£X] to [£Y] a month depending on how much support you actually need. I'd rather not throw a number at you before I know if I can even help though. Can I ask you two quick things first?

Never

Say "it depends" and nothing else. Say "let's jump on a call and I'll explain" as your only answer. Both feel evasive and both cost you the lead.

Play 08 · Money

No seriously, just tell me the price

What it usually means

They asked twice. They have now decided you're being slippery, and they're right. Trust is draining in real time.

Do this

Answer it straight, immediately, with a number. Then offer the context as a separate, optional thing.

Straight answer

Course — [£X] a month, minimum [3] months. That's [what's included]. Want me to break down what that actually looks like week to week?

Rule

Never dodge a price question twice. One deferral with a range is professional. Two is evasive, and people who feel handled do not buy.

Play 09 · Money

That's too expensive

What it usually means

One of two completely different things, and you cannot respond well until you know which. Either the money genuinely isn't there right now, or they aren't convinced it's worth that much yet. The first is a budget fact. The second is a value conversation.

Do this

Separate the two with one question, asked without defensiveness.

The split

Totally fair. Just so I know which it is — is it more that the number's out of reach at the minute, or that you're not convinced it's worth it yet? Genuinely no wrong answer.

If it's out of reach

Be honest and be kind. Offer the lower-tier option only if you genuinely run one. Otherwise: "Honestly then, now's not the time and that's completely fine. Can I check back in [month]? In the meantime here's [genuinely useful free thing]."

If it's a value question

That belongs on the call, not in a DM. "That's a completely fair thing to want to test. That's exactly what the call's for — I'll walk you through what the first month actually looks like and you can decide from there."

Never

Suggest credit cards, buy-now-pay-later, or borrowing. Never use "you're worth the investment" as a pressure line. Pushing someone into a payment they can't afford creates a refund, a chargeback and a very public review.

Play 10 · Money

Can you do me a discount?

What it usually means

Often a habit rather than a real barrier — plenty of people ask reflexively. Sometimes it's play 09 wearing a different hat.

Do this

Hold the price, and offer less scope rather than a lower price for the same thing. Discounting the same service teaches everyone the price was never real — including the clients who paid it.

Hold the line, warmly

I keep the price the same for everyone — wouldn't be fair on the people already paying it. What I can do is [smaller option: fewer check-ins / training-only / shorter block] at [£Y]. Want me to explain how that one works?

Play 11 · Money

Can I pay in instalments / pay next month?

What it usually means

Usually a genuine cash-flow timing issue, not a value objection. Often very close to a yes.

Do this

Offer only what you actually operate. If you have a split-payment option, name it plainly with the real total. If you don't, offer a start date that lines up with their payday instead — and then honour it.

Payday start

I don't run instalments, but there's no reason we can't start on [date after payday]. I'll hold you a spot and send everything over the day before. Does that work?

Never

Invent a payment plan on the spot that your systems can't take, or quote a split total that hides extra cost. Always state the full amount they'll pay.

Play 12 · Timing

I'll have a think about it

What it usually means

There is one specific unresolved thing and they don't want to say it out loud. It is nearly always money, time, or "will this actually work for someone like me". Your job is to find out which — not to overcome it.

Do this

Give them three named doors. People will pick a door far more readily than they'll volunteer an objection.

Three doors

Course — what's the bit you want to think over? The money, the time it takes, or whether it'll actually work for you? Whichever it is I'd rather just talk about it straight.

Then

Whatever they name, address that one thing honestly, then agree a specific day to speak again. "Let me know!" is where deals go to die — replace it with "Shall I check in Thursday?"

Play 13 · Timing

I'm too busy at the minute

What it usually means

Either genuinely swamped this fortnight, or "this isn't a priority" said politely. Those need different responses, so don't assume.

Do this

Acknowledge it honestly — then separate short-term chaos from a genuine seasonal no.

Busy, split

Makes sense — and honestly being busy is why most people bring me in, the whole point is that it fits around everything else. But genuinely: is this a "not this fortnight" or a "not this year"? I'd rather know so I stop bugging you.

Never

Argue with their schedule, or imply they'd find the time if they cared enough. Telling someone their priorities are wrong has never once resulted in a sale.

Play 14 · Timing

Ask me again after my holiday / in January

What it usually means

Sometimes a real date, sometimes a soft no. Either way, treating it as real costs you nothing and wins you the ones who meant it.

Do this

Pin the exact date, say you'll message then, put it in your diary, and actually do it. Coaches who follow up on the promised day close a meaningful share of these.

Pin the date

No problem. When are you back — [date]? I'll put a note in my diary and message you the week after. Enjoy it 👍

The promised follow-up

Hey [name] — said I'd message you when you were back. How was [the holiday/event]? Still fancy getting stuck into [goal]?

Play 15 · Timing

I need to speak to my partner first

What it usually means

Usually completely genuine — it's a shared household expense and a shared time commitment. Occasionally it's a polite exit. Respect it either way; a partner who feels bypassed will veto on principle.

Do this

Support the conversation instead of trying to get around it. Arm them with the two or three things their partner will actually ask about: cost, time commitment, and what changes at home.

Arm them

Completely get it. So you've got the actual numbers for them: it's [£X] a month, about [X] hours a week, and the main change at home is [the realistic one]. Happy to jump on a quick call with both of you if that's easier. When are you speaking to them?

Never

"It's your body, it's your decision" or anything that nudges them to go around their partner. It's insulting, it's manipulative, and it loses the deal roughly every time.

Play 16 · Trust

I've had a coach before and it didn't work

What it usually means

Gold, not an objection. They've already proven they'll pay for coaching. You just need to find out what broke last time so you can say honestly whether you'd do it differently.

Do this

Get curious, and let them talk. This is the single best qualifying question you'll ever ask.

Dig in

That's really useful to know — what went wrong? Was it the plan itself, or more that you didn't get the support you needed around it?

Then

Name specifically how you work differently on that exact thing. If you don't actually work differently, say so and tell them you might not be the fix — you'll lose one sale and gain a reputation.

Never

Slag off the other coach. It reads as insecure, and the client often still likes them.

Play 17 · Trust

Have you worked with anyone like me?

What it usually means

"I'm worried I'm a special case." Usually it's about age, a body type, shift work, an injury, or having a lot to lose.

Do this

Give one specific, relevant, true example — with the detail that makes it land. One real story beats ten vague claims.

One real example

Yeah — [first name or "a client"], [similar situation]. [What specifically changed and roughly how long it took.] Everyone's different, but the pattern's usually the same.

Never

Invent a client. Share a photo, name or result without written permission. Use someone else's transformation images. Every one of those is a sackable, potentially legal problem, and screenshots last forever.

If the honest answer is no

Say so, and say what you'd do about it: "Not with that exact thing, no. What I'd want to do is [specific plan], and if I thought you needed someone more specialist I'd tell you."

Play 18 · Trust

How do I know this isn't a scam?

What it usually means

Reasonable caution, often from someone who has been burned online before. Do not take it personally and do not get defensive — defensiveness is exactly what they're testing for.

Do this

Be boringly transparent. Real name, real business, real terms, no payment until after a conversation.

Transparency

Completely fair thing to ask. I'm [full name], I coach under [business name] — [website]. Nothing gets paid before we've spoken, you'll get terms in writing, and [refund/cancellation policy in one line]. Have a look and ask me anything.

Play 19 · Trust

They keep asking for free advice and never engage with anything else

What it usually means

Either they don't know coaching is an option, or they've decided your inbox is a free service. Answer once generously — the second is only clear after you've tested the first.

Do this

Give one genuinely good answer. Then transition once, cleanly. If they take the answer and ask another question, cap it warmly.

Answer, then transition

[Genuinely useful answer in 2–3 lines.] That'll get you moving. Out of interest — are you piecing this together yourself, or would you rather someone just built it around you?

The kind cap

Happy to keep answering the odd thing, but this is properly the stuff I get paid for so I can't build you a plan over DM 😅 If you ever want it done properly, say the word and I'll sort you out.

Play 20 · Logistics

Do you do in-person? I'd prefer face to face

What it usually means

Usually not a location preference at heart — it's a worry about accountability and about technique. Address the real concern.

Do this

Answer the factual question first, then speak to the concern underneath it. Never oversell online as identical to in-person; name honestly what it does better and what it doesn't.

Online, honestly

[Straight answer on whether you do in-person.] What most people are actually worried about is form and accountability — the way I handle that is [video form checks / weekly calls / daily check-ins]. What online does better is you get me every week rather than for one hour. What it can't do is physically move your elbow — so I'm fussy about video.

Play 21 · Logistics

Can you just send me a programme?

What it usually means

They believe the missing piece is information. Based on everything they've told you, it usually isn't — but saying "you don't know what you need" is not the move.

Do this

Sell what you actually sell. If you have a programme-only product, offer it honestly. If you don't, explain the gap between what a programme does and what they described, without being sniffy about it.

Programme vs coaching

I can — but going off what you said earlier, the plan isn't really the bit that's broken. You said you're fine for three weeks then one bad week knocks you off. A PDF doesn't fix that, a person does. [If you sell programmes: "Happy to sell you the plan on its own at £X if you'd rather start there."]

Play 22 · Logistics

I'm in a completely different timezone

What it usually means

A practical question about whether you'll actually be available to them.

Do this

Confirm what you can genuinely commit to and never promise availability you won't deliver. Offer times in their local time, and always write the timezone out.

Timezone booking

No problem — where are you based? I'll send times in your local time. For day-to-day, check-ins are [async/voice note] so the gap doesn't matter, and calls I'd do [your realistic window].

Play 23 · Logistics

They ask something you genuinely don't know the answer to

What it usually means

Nothing bad. It happens constantly and it is not a test.

Do this

Say you don't know, say when you'll come back, then come back. Coaches lose far more deals by bluffing than by admitting a gap — and a confident wrong answer about training or nutrition can genuinely hurt someone.

Honest gap

Honest answer — I'm not sure off the top of my head and I don't want to guess at something that matters. Let me check properly and come back to you tonight.

Play 24 · Sensitive

They turn out to be under 18

What it matters

Safeguarding, insurance and, in many places, law. This is not a judgement call to make on the fly.

Do this

Stop the sales conversation. Do not coach a minor without a parent or guardian involved and consenting, and follow whatever your insurer and local rules require. Be warm — they're a kid who wants to get fitter, which is a good thing.

Under 18

Ah — I can't take anyone under 18 on without a parent or guardian involved, that's just how it has to work. Genuinely though, fair play for taking it seriously. If a parent wants to chat, they're very welcome to message me.

Never

Continue a one-to-one sales conversation with a minor, take payment from a minor, or discuss weight loss, calorie targets or body composition with them.

Play 25 · Sensitive

You see possible signs of disordered eating

Signs to watch for

Requests for extreme deficits or very rapid loss. Mentions of purging, laxatives, fasting for days, or "earning" food. Intense body-hate language. Already very lean and wanting to lose more. Fixation on a number that doesn't match what they describe of themselves.

Do this

Leave sales mode entirely and do not return to it in this conversation. Respond as a person, don't diagnose, don't lecture, and point them toward appropriate support. This is the one situation where the right business decision and the right human decision are identical: do not take this sale.

Step out of sales

I'm going to be straight with you because I'd rather be honest than get a client. What you're describing isn't something I'd coach — taking it on would risk making things worse for you, and I'm not the right person for it. Talking to your GP or a specialist about it would be a genuinely good move. There's real support out there and it does help. I'm not disappearing on you, I'm just not the right fit for this.

Never

Sell them a fat-loss package. Give them a calorie target. Tell them they "just need discipline". Diagnose anything. And never continue the pitch after this message — flag it to your lead coach instead.

Play 26 · Sensitive

They disclose an injury, a medical condition, medication, or pregnancy

Why it matters

Scope of practice. A personal training or nutrition qualification does not make you a physiotherapist, a doctor or a registered dietitian, and acting like one is how coaches get sued and clients get hurt.

Do this

Thank them for telling you, don't speculate about the condition, and be clear about where your lane ends. Then either work alongside their clinician with clearance, or refer out.

Scope of practice

Thanks for flagging that — good that you did. I'm not going to guess at anything medical, that's not my lane. What I'd normally do is work around it with clearance from [your GP / physio / consultant], and stay in touch with them if they're happy to. Have you spoken to them about training?

Never

Say "that's fine, we'll train around it" without clearance. Comment on their medication. Suggest they stop or change a prescription. Claim training will treat, cure or manage a diagnosed condition.

Play 27 · Sensitive

The conversation turns flirty or inappropriate

What it usually means

Doesn't matter. The response is the same regardless of intent.

Do this

One clear, unapologetic boundary. No joke to soften it, no laughing emoji — ambiguity gets read as an invitation. If it continues at all: screenshot, block, report, and tell your lead coach.

The boundary

I'm going to keep this professional — that's not what this is. If you want to talk training I'm happy to, otherwise I'll leave it here.

Never

Play along to keep a sale alive. Reply with "haha 😂" and change the subject. Keep the conversation going out of politeness. No deal is worth this.

Play 28 · Sensitive

They're rude, sarcastic, or aggressive

What it usually means

Almost always about their day, not about you. Occasionally someone fishing for a reaction to screenshot.

Do this

Never match it. One neutral, gracious line, then leave. Write every message assuming it will be screenshotted, because the hostile ones usually are.

Exit gracefully

All good — I'll leave you to it. If you ever do want a hand with it, you know where I am 👍

Play 29 · Sensitive

They disclose something heavy — grief, a breakup, mental health

What it usually means

They're telling you the real reason training fell apart. It's trust, and it deserves to be treated as trust rather than as an opening.

Do this

Be a person. Acknowledge it properly and make no offer in that message. Not a soft one. If it's right to pick the conversation back up, do it days later and let them lead.

Human first

Ah mate, I'm really sorry. That's a lot to be carrying and training is rightly the last thing on the list right now. No agenda from me at all — if you fancy picking it back up down the line just shout.

Never

Use it as leverage ("training would really help with that right now"). Even when it's true, saying it in that moment is transparently self-serving and people remember it.

Play 30 · After the call

They no-show the booked call

What it usually means

About half are genuine — work overran, kid kicked off, phone on silent. The other half got cold feet. Assume the first out loud, plan for the second.

Do this

Message within ten minutes, assume the best, and offer one reschedule with two specific times.

No-show · +10 min

No worries if something's come up — I know how it goes. Want to grab [tomorrow 6:40pm] or [Thursday 1:10pm] instead?

If they no-show twice

Stop booking calls. Move them to the follow-up ladder. Two no-shows means the intent isn't there yet, and a third booking just clutters your calendar.

Play 31 · After the call

They said yes on the call, then went silent on payment

What it usually means

Usually friction or second thoughts, not dishonesty. The link didn't work, the card got declined, they wanted to check the account, or the excitement faded on the drive home.

Do this

Remove friction first, before you ever apply any pressure. Two messages maximum, then let it breathe.

Message 1 · same day

Hey [name] — did the link come through alright? Shout if anything's playing up and I'll sort it another way.

Message 2 · +48h

No stress either way — has anything changed since we spoke? Completely fine if you've had second thoughts, I'd just rather know than keep the spot held.

Never

Invent a deadline that doesn't exist, or claim the price is going up when it isn't. Fake urgency is the single most common reason people feel scammed by fitness coaches.

Play 32 · After the call

They said no on the call

What it usually means

Not now. It very rarely means never — a meaningful share of "no" leads buy within the year, from the coach who took the no well.

Do this

Thank them properly, leave one genuinely useful thing behind, and ask permission to check back at a specific point. Then hold to it.

Take the no well

Appreciate you being straight with me, genuinely. Based on what you said, the one thing I'd actually do if I were you is [one specific, useful action]. Alright if I check in around [60 days / specific month] to see how you're getting on? No pitch, just curious.

Never

Go cold, get short, or stop replying once the money's off the table. Prospects notice instantly, and that's the moment they decide whether to ever recommend you.

08

The follow-up ladder

For a qualified lead who has gone quiet. Seven touches over thirty days, each with a different job. The point of varying the angle is simple: if four messages in a row all say "are you still interested", you've given them the same reason to ignore you four times.

Rule

Never send two rungs of this ladder in the same day, and never send a rung immediately after an unanswered one. If they reply at any point, you leave the ladder and go back to the conversation.

Day 0 · 4–6 hours later

The easy bump

Same-day, light, and re-asks your question in a form that takes five seconds to answer.

Quick one so I don't leave you hanging — was it more [option A] or [option B]?
Day 1

Value, with no ask attached

Send one thing that is genuinely useful for the exact problem they described. No question, no pitch. This resets the relationship from "someone selling me" to "someone helping me".

Not chasing you — this just made me think of what you said about [their problem]: [link / tip / short voice note]. Might be useful.
Day 3

The direct one

Light but unambiguous. Gives them a clean way to park it without feeling rude.

Did you want to park this for now or pick it back up? Either's completely fine, just don't want to be that guy messaging into the void 😅
Day 7

The reframe

Come at the problem from a new angle, or share a relevant (real, permitted) client outcome that maps onto their situation.

Random — just had a client hit [specific thing] and she started in almost exactly the spot you described. Made me think of you. How's it going?
Day 14

The permission close

The most effective message in the ladder. Asking permission to stop very often gets a reply, because it's easy to answer and it's disarmingly honest. Mean it.

Totally fine if the timing's off — want me to stop bringing it up? Genuinely no hard feelings either way.
Day 30

The reset

A new month, a natural restart point, no reference to the previous silence.

New month. Is [their goal] still on the list, or has it dropped off?
After day 30 · nurture only

Stop DMing. Stay visible.

Move them out of active follow-up. Watch their stories, react genuinely, reply when they post something relevant. No more direct messages unless they message you. A good number of leads come back on their own from exactly here — but only if you left on good terms.

The small stuff that decides whether you read as a person or as a pipeline.

When to use a voice note

  • Good: explaining something with nuance that would take four paragraphs to type.
  • Good: after a hard objection, where tone does the work that text can't.
  • Good: answering a technical question about their training.
  • Bad: the first message. The pitch. Anything containing a price. Anything over a minute.

Structure for a good one: name them, name the thing they said, give the answer, ask one question, stop. Record it once — a polished second take sounds like an advert.

Writing style

  • Write how you talk. If you wouldn't say it out loud in a gym, don't type it.
  • Contractions always. "I'd", "you're", "that's" — not "I would", "you are".
  • Cut the first sentence of anything you write. It's almost always throat-clearing.
  • No jargon until they use it first. "Progressive overload" and "TDEE" mean nothing to most people and quietly make them feel stupid.
  • Never open with "Hope you're well!" — it marks the message as business before they've read a word.
