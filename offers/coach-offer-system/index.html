The assumptions behind these numbers
  • A client must be worth 3× what you pay to get them. Below that there is nothing left for delivery, software, your team, or you.
  • A booked call costs $100–400. That is the honest range, and it is the dial that matters most. It also moves with your offer: premium positioning buys dearer leads, so raising your price raises this number too. Do not plan on the bottom of the range.
  • “Works” means 50% headroom. Break-even on lead cost is a coin flip, not a business.
  • Structure ceilings assume a competent operator — 70% of booked calls showing up, 30% of held calls closing. Worse than that and every ceiling on this page drops proportionally.
02

Five structures that hold

Every one of these is in the field right now. Each shows its ceiling — the most a booked call can cost before the structure stops working. Pick for the traffic you will actually buy, not for the biggest headline number.

The Minimum Commitment

$600/mo · 4 months · no exit

For the coach whose results are real but whose clients quit before the results arrive.

Term
Four months, mandatory. Not a discount for committing — the only way to buy.
Pay in full
10% off. Payment plans get no discount, ever.
Cash per client
$2,400, or $2,160 paid up front.
Ceiling
$168 per booked call. Survives cheap traffic, dies above $110.
Why it works: body composition takes 12–16 weeks to show. A month-to-month client quits in week six, blames you, and leaves without a testimonial. The commitment isn’t for your cash flow, it’s so the programme has long enough to work.

The Deposit Hybrid

$2,500 up front + $300/mo · 6 months

For premium positioning, and for funding your own ad spend.

Structure
Large deposit at signing, then modest monthly for the term.
Cash per client
$4,300 over six months, $2,500 of it in week one.
Needs
A buyer with real income. This does not work on a budget audience.
Ceiling
$301 per booked call. Comfortable anywhere up to $200.
Why it works: the deposit pays for the next client’s acquisition. It is the only structure on this list that lets you scale ad spend without financing it from savings — which is exactly why it is worth the harder sales conversation.

The Recurring Renewal

$2,000 every 6 months

For group programmes currently sold as lifetime access.

The fix
Delete lifetime access. Sell a six-month term that renews.
Cash per client
$4,000 per year, continuing, instead of $4,000 once.
Migration
Grandfather existing buyers. Change it for new ones only.
Ceiling
$210 per booked call, assuming one renewal on average.
Why it works: lifetime access caps your business at the number of humans you can find. It also quietly obliges you to serve people forever on revenue you already spent. Renewal turns one good month into a compounding asset.

The Paid-In-Full Intensive

$6,000 · 2 months · PIF only

For a done-with-you build where the outcome is an asset, not a habit.

Term
Short and dense. Two months, paid in full, no plan offered.
Deliverable
Something that still exists when you stop — a funnel, a system, an ad account.
Cash per client
$6,000 on day one.
Ceiling
$420 per booked call. The only structure here that takes the top of the range.
Why it works: a short term with a tangible artefact justifies full payment in a way an open-ended coaching relationship never will. Price on the asset they keep, not the hours you spend.

The Recoup Guarantee

Any price · 12 weeks · we stay until you break even

For a prospect who has already been burned by an agency.

Promise
If they haven’t made their investment back by the end of the term, you keep working free.
Not
A refund. You are selling more work, not giving money back.
Requires
Delivery you genuinely control, and an honest view of your own close rate.
Ceiling
Lifts whatever structure it sits on. Moving close rate from 30% to 40% raises every ceiling above by about a third.
Why it works: it transfers the risk without touching the price, and it disqualifies you from selling to people you cannot actually help — which is the real reason it raises close rates. Only offer it where you would happily do the extra weeks.
03

Qualifying on money, early

The most expensive call you can run is a brilliant 50-minute pitch to somebody who was never going to be able to pay. Five beats, in order, inside the first ten minutes.

Beat 1 — set the frame before anything else
“Before I ask you anything, let me tell you how this works. I’m going to ask what you’ve tried and what it cost you. If I don’t think I can get you where you want to go, I’ll tell you on this call and we’ll stop. Fair?”

Earns permission to ask uncomfortable questions, and signals you are willing to not sell. Both do more for the close than any pitch.

Beat 2 — go past the surface goal
“You said you want to lose thirty pounds. What happens if you’re in the exact same body twelve months from now? Walk me through what that actually costs you.”

The stated goal is never the reason. People buy from the cost of staying the same, and they cannot tell you that cost until you make them say it out loud.

Beat 3 — price the problem before you price the offer
“What have you spent trying to fix this so far? Programmes, gyms, apps, coaches — roughly, all in.”

Establishes they already spend on this, and gives you their real number. Somebody who has burned $4,000 on failed attempts is not frightened by $2,400.

Beat 4 — qualify the money, out loud
“Programmes like this run two to three thousand for the full term. If it’s genuinely the thing that fixes this — is that a number you can do, or is that out of reach right now? Either answer is fine, I just don’t want to waste your afternoon.”

This is the beat nearly everyone skips, and skipping it is what produces a calendar full of “let me think about it.” Ask it by minute ten, every time.

Beat 5 — sell the outcome, never the apparatus
“You’re not buying check-ins and a spreadsheet. You’re buying being the person who doesn’t think about this any more. The four months is just how long that takes.”

Features invite comparison shopping; outcomes don’t. The term length stops being a cost and becomes the reason it works.
