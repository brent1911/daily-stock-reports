Section 01

Build the offer

Before the ceiling, before tracking, before a single ad. Ads amplify an offer — they never create one. A great offer with mediocre ads makes money; perfect ads pointed at a weak offer just lose it faster.

Eight parts. Fill each one in, in order, and the end of this section assembles them into a single statement you'll use in every ad, page and call that follows.

1. Who, exactly

Narrower than feels comfortable. "Busy professionals" is not an avatar — it's a way of avoiding the choice.

Fill in

I work with [AGE RANGE] [GENDER, IF IT'S TRUE] who are [JOB OR LIFE SITUATION] and [THE CONSTRAINT THEY LIVE WITH].

I work with 30–45 year old women who are nurses on rotating shifts and can't train at the same time two days running.
Why narrow wins on every metric at once

A narrow avatar writes your ad copy for you, raises click-through because the reader recognises themselves, raises close rate because you've solved this exact problem before, and lets you charge more because you're a specialist rather than a generalist.

The fear is that you shrink your market. In practice you are picking the slice you already convert best, and paid traffic will find you more of them than you could ever reach organically.

2. The promise

Outcome, timeframe, and the thing they dread that you remove. All three, or it isn't a promise.

Fill in

[OUTCOME THEY'D NOTICE DAY TO DAY] in [WEEKS] weeks, without [THE THING THEY DREAD].

Get through a full shift without the 3pm crash, in 12 weeks, without 5am starts or giving up wine.
Make the outcome felt, not numeric. "Lose 20lbs" is a weaker promise than "get your energy back" — it's less emotionally specific, easier to disprove, and it's the phrasing that gets your ads rejected. Pick something they'd notice on a Tuesday.

Be honest about the timeframe. Body composition changes show up around 12–16 weeks. Promising it in 6 sells the first month and loses the client in the third.

3. The mechanism — the part that sets your price

This is the single biggest lever in the whole section. The mechanism is why yours works when the last three things didn't. Without one you're selling "coaching", which is a commodity priced by the hour. With one you're selling a specific method, which is priced by the outcome.

ArchetypeThe claimFits a coach who…
SequencingWe fix [X] before touching [Y] — doing it the other way round is why it failed beforeHas a strong opinion about order
Constraint-fitBuilt around [THEIR REAL CONSTRAINT], not adapted to it afterwardsServes one specific lifestyle
Minimum dose[N] sessions of [N] minutes. Everything else was optionalWorks with time-poor clients
Adjustment cycleWe change the plan every [N] days based on [WHAT YOU MEASURE]Is systematic and data-led
Root causeMost [WHO] blame [WRONG CAUSE]. It's actually [REAL CAUSE]Has a contrarian, defensible view
Fill in — one sentence, no jargon

Most [WHO] think the problem is [WHAT THEY BLAME]. It's actually [THE REAL MECHANISM], which is why [WHAT THEY KEEP TRYING] makes it worse. So we [WHAT YOU DO INSTEAD] first.

Most shift workers think the problem is willpower. It's actually that rotating sleep wrecks appetite signalling, which is why eating less makes the next week worse. So we fix sleep anchors and protein floor before we touch calories at all.
Test it like this

Say your mechanism out loud to someone outside fitness. If they can repeat it back, it's a mechanism. If they say "so… personal training?", you don't have one yet — and no amount of ad spend will fix that.

You almost certainly already have one. It's the thing you explain on every sales call and have never written down.

4. What they actually get

Five lines maximum, each tied to the mechanism. Deliverables that don't serve the mechanism are padding, and experienced buyers read padding as weakness.

Fill in

• [THE TRAINING: format, frequency, where]
• [THE NUTRITION: structure, not a meal plan]
• [THE CHECK-IN: how often, what happens in it]
• [ACCESS: how they reach you, and the honest response time]
• [THE ADJUSTMENT: what changes, and how often]

State the access honestly. "WhatsApp, answered within a day on weekdays" keeps clients happier than "24/7 support" that you resent by week three.

5. The structure

How the money is arranged, which turns out to matter more than the number itself. Each structure can absorb a different amount of traffic cost before the economics break:

StructureShapeClient valueCan afford per booked call
Month-to-month$150/mo, cancel anytime$450$32
Minimum commitment$600/mo × 4 months$2,400$168
Recurring renewal$2,000 per 6 months$3,000$210
Deposit hybrid$2,500 up front + $300 × 6$4,300$301
Paid in full$6,000 for 2 months, intensive$6,000$420
Month-to-month cannot run paid ads. At all. It can afford $32 per booked call and nothing you could buy comes close. If that's your current structure, changing it is the highest-value thing in this entire guide — worth more than any targeting or creative decision you will ever make.

Three rules when picking:

  1. Match the term to the result. If the promise takes 12–16 weeks, the commitment is four months. Selling month-to-month on a twelve-week promise guarantees people leave before it works.
  2. Take money up front where you honestly can. Up-front cash funds the ad spend that fills the next slot, and clients who pay more up front show up more.
  3. Never discount the payment plan. Price it above the paid-in-full total. The plan removes a cash objection; it isn't the better deal.

6. The price

Fill in

Paid in full: [$____]  ·  Plan: [$____] × [__] months  ·  Total if plan: [$____]

Minimum commitment: [__] months

Two sanity checks before you move on:

  • Is this worth 10× the price to them? If someone pays $2,400 to stop crashing at 3pm every shift for the next decade, that's an easy yes. If you can't make that case, the promise is too small — fix the promise, not the price.
  • Would you pay it? If you'd hesitate, you'll communicate that hesitation on every call, and prospects read it instantly.

7. The guarantee

A guarantee isn't softness — it's a lever on close rate, and close rate multiplies straight through to what you can afford to pay for traffic.

What a guarantee is actually worth

Lifting close rate from 30% to 40% raises every ceiling in the table above by about a third, because it cuts the booked calls needed per signed client from 4.8 to 3.6.

That is the same effect as making your traffic a third cheaper — achieved by changing a sentence on your offer rather than by becoming better at Meta ads.

Guarantee the work, never the outcome. You control whether you deliver; you don't control whether they do.

Three that hold up

Recoup. Do [THE DEFINED WORK] for [N] weeks and if you haven't [THE MINIMUM OUTCOME], I coach you free until you do.

Process. Complete every check-in for [N] weeks and if you can honestly say you got nothing, full refund.

First two weeks. If it isn't what I described within 14 days, full refund, no argument.

The recoup version is strongest: it costs you time rather than money, it only triggers for people who did the work, and "I'll keep going until it works" is the most reassuring sentence a sceptical buyer can hear.

8. What it isn't

Two or three lines. Saying this out loud raises conversion among the people who do fit, and it is the cheapest filter you will ever install.

Fill in

This isn't for you if you want [WHAT YOU DON'T DO], if you're after [THE SHORTCUT YOU REFUSE], or if you can't commit [THE MINIMUM REALISTIC INPUT].

Assemble it

One sentence. This becomes your ad hook, your landing page headline, and the first thing you say on every call.

Your offer statement

I help [WHO] [OUTCOME] in [TIMEFRAME] through [MECHANISM], without [WHAT THEY DREAD] — for [PRICE] over [TERM], guaranteed by [GUARANTEE].

I help shift-working nurses get through a full shift without the 3pm crash in 12 weeks, by fixing sleep anchors and protein before touching calories, without 5am starts — for $600 a month over four months, and if they do the check-ins and nothing changes I keep coaching free until it does.

The stress test

Eight questions. Any "no" is cheaper to fix now than after $600 of traffic.

  • Can you say the whole offer in one sentence, without notes?
  • Does the promise have a timeframe you'd defend?
  • Could a stranger repeat your mechanism back to you?
  • Does every deliverable serve that mechanism?
  • Does the term match how long the result actually takes?
  • Would you pay this price yourself?
  • Can you name who it's not for?
  • Does your structure clear $150 per booked call? (Section 2 proves it)
Don't move on until the last one is yes. Section 2 is where you test it with your own numbers, and it is the gate on every dollar you spend after this.
Section 02

Test the offer: your ceiling

You have an offer. Now find out whether it can survive paid traffic — the most you can afford to pay for one booked call. If you skip this, everything downstream is guesswork.

Ceiling = Show % × Close % × (Client value ÷ 3)

The divide-by-three is the rule that keeps you solvent: a client has to be worth at least three times what you pay to acquire them. Anything tighter and one refund or one bad month wipes the account.

Fill this in

A — What one client pays you in total$________
B — Of people who book, how many show up_____ %
C — Of people who show, how many buy_____ %
D — Your ceiling: B × C × (A ÷ 3)$________

Don't have B and C? Use 70% and 30%. Those are competent-operator numbers, not best case. If your real numbers come in lower, your ceiling drops with them.

Those five structures from Section 1 carry very different ceilings — $32 at month-to-month, $420 paid in full. Run your own numbers above and compare them to the structure you picked.

If you need to raise your ceiling

Four levers, in the order they are easiest to move:

  1. Add an upfront payment. $1,500 down then monthly beats pure monthly on day-one cash and on retention.
  2. Extend the minimum term. Four months instead of month-to-month. Body composition results take 12–16 weeks; a one-month offer promises something it can't deliver.
  3. Raise the price. The fastest lever and the one most coaches avoid. A 20% increase moves your ceiling 20%.
  4. Fix show rate before close rate. Easier to move, and Section 13 is entirely about it.

Don't model retention past 12 months. You can't forecast it honestly, and building a budget on it is how accounts go broke slowly.

Section 03

Choose your funnel

Two ways to get a call booked. They cost different amounts, need different budgets, and fail for different reasons. Pick deliberately — most coaches drift into one by accident and never find out the other suited them better.

Path A — the DM funnel

Ad → they tap Send message → you have a conversation → you offer two times → call booked.

A human qualifies before anyone touches a calendar. Cheap conversations, high volume, and it needs someone answering messages inside five minutes.

Path B — the call funnel

Ad → landing page → they watch or read → application form → calendar → call booked.

The page and the form do the qualifying. Nobody touches it until the call itself. More expensive per booking, and it runs while you sleep.

What each actually costs

The show-rate gap is the thing nobody tells you

Path A shows up better — often 20 points better — for one reason: they already spoke to a person. A booking made after a real conversation is an appointment with someone you've met. A booking made from a form is a line in a calendar.

So compare the paths on cost per call held, never on cost per call booked. On the booked number Path A looks twice as good; on the held number it often looks three times as good.

If you're under $50/day, this isn't a choice. Run Path A. The call funnel needs volume to work at all, for the reason below.

Why the call funnel needs more budget

Meta optimises properly once an ad set gets roughly 50 conversion events a week. Below that it never leaves the learning phase and delivery stays erratic and expensive.

PathEvent being optimisedTypical costSpend needed for 50/week
A — DMConversation started$4–12$30–85/day
B — optimise for form submitLead$15–40$105–285/day
B — optimise for bookingSchedule$60–200$430+/day

At $20/day a DM campaign gets 12–35 conversations a week — not ideal, but enough to move. A call funnel optimising for bookings gets two or three. It will never learn.

So if you run Path B under $150/day, optimise for the form submit (Lead), not the booking (Schedule). You trade some lead quality for an algorithm that actually works. Switch to Schedule once you're past 50 bookings a week.

The sequence that works best

If you have the time, don't choose — sequence. Run Path A first and let it build Path B for you:

  1. Weeks 1–4: Path A only. $20/day. Cheap conversations, and every one teaches you something.
  2. Keep a note of the objections. Every "how much is it", every "I don't have time", every reason someone went quiet. This is free market research most coaches throw away.
  3. Weeks 5–6: write the landing page from those notes. Your DM transcripts are the copy. The objection you answered nineteen times is your headline.
  4. Week 7+: launch Path B alongside. Don't turn Path A off — run both and compare cost per call held.
Why this order beats starting with a landing page

A landing page written before you've had a hundred DM conversations is a guess about what your market objects to. A landing page written after them is a transcript.

Coaches who start with Path B usually spend $1,500 learning what forty DM conversations would have told them for $300.

Running both at once

Once both work, the standard structure is:

  • Cold traffic → Path A. Cheaper to learn on, and the conversation handles scepticism a page can't.
  • Warm and retargeting → Path B. Anyone who engaged, watched 50% of a video, or hit the page and didn't book. They already know you, so the page converts far better than it does cold.
  • Separate campaigns, separate budgets. Never the same campaign. You cannot read the numbers if they're mixed.

Which sections apply to you: Path A runs on Section 8 and Section 9. Path B runs on Section 10 and Section 11. Everything from Section 12 onward — the calendar, the show-up system, optimization, the scorecard — applies to both.

Section 05

Tracking

Do this before you write a single ad. An account that learns from day one is worth more than a prettier ad, and an account that was never instrumented can never tell you what a call costs.

Five things, in this order. Each has a test. Don't move on until the test passes.

#InstallThe test that proves it
1Pixel on every pageMeta Pixel Helper shows it firing on your offer page
2Domain verifiedGreen tick in Business settings → Brand safety
3Lead or Schedule event on booking confirmationBook a test call yourself. Event appears in Events Manager within 20 minutes
4Conversions APIEvent shows Server as well as Browser in Events Manager
5Event prioritisedYour booking event sits at position 1 in Aggregated Event Measurement
Why the Conversions API is not optional

Browser-only tracking loses a meaningful share of conversions to ad blockers, privacy settings and iOS. Those conversions still happen — Meta just never learns from them, so it keeps optimising on partial data.

Most booking tools (GoHighLevel, Calendly, Hubspot) have a native Meta CAPI integration. Turn it on. It is a settings toggle, not a development project.

Attach a value to the booking

Send your booking event with a value, not bare. Use client value × show % × close % — the expected revenue of one booked call.

Worked example

$2,400 client value × 0.70 show × 0.30 close = $504 per booked call.

Send value: 504 and currency: USD with every Schedule event.

This is what lets Meta report a return figure at all, and it is why most coaches' accounts show nothing in that column. It takes two minutes and almost nobody does it.

The test transaction

  1. Open your offer page in a private window
  2. Book a call using a real email and phone
  3. Open Events Manager → Test events
  4. Confirm the event lands, carries a value, and shows both Browser and Server
  5. Cancel the test booking
Do not skip this. Watching one event land end to end is the only proof that any of the above actually works. Assuming it works is how coaches spend $2,000 before discovering nothing was recorded.
Section 06

Ad scripts

Six frameworks. Replace the bracketed parts and you have a running ad. Build at least eight before you launch — one ad is a guess, eight is a test.

Before you write: the first two seconds carry everything. Never open with "Hey guys" or your own name. Open with the reader's situation.

1. The specific result

Your strongest ad, every time. Works because it is a claim about a named person, not about the reader's body.

Script

[FIRST NAME] is a [AGE]-year-old [JOB] with [CONSTRAINT — two kids, 60-hour weeks, shift work].

Twelve weeks ago she couldn't [SPECIFIC THING SHE COULDN'T DO].

Last week she [SPECIFIC THING SHE DID].

She trains [NUMBER] times a week for [MINUTES] minutes. No [THING THEY FEAR — meal prep Sundays, 5am starts, giving up wine].

I coach [WHO YOU WORK WITH] in [CITY / ONLINE]. If you want the same, send me the word [KEYWORD] and I'll show you what her plan looked like.

Sarah is a 41-year-old nurse with two kids and rotating night shifts. Twelve weeks ago she couldn't get through a shift without three coffees and a crash at 3pm. Last week she ran her first 5k. She trains three times a week for 40 minutes. No 5am starts, no giving up wine. Send me the word ENERGY and I'll show you her plan.

2. Problem, agitate, solve

Script

You're [DOING THE RIGHT THING] and still [NOT GETTING THE RESULT].

So you try harder. [THING THEY ESCALATE TO]. Then [NEXT THING]. Three weeks later you're [WHERE THEY END UP] and quietly wondering if it's just you.

It isn't. It's that [THE ACTUAL MECHANISM THEY'RE MISSING].

I've walked [NUMBER] [WHO] through fixing exactly that. Send [KEYWORD] and I'll tell you where to start.

You're in the gym five days a week and the scale hasn't moved since February. So you add cardio. Then you cut carbs. Three weeks later you're exhausted, eating 1,200 calories, and quietly wondering if it's just you. It isn't. It's that you've been under-eating protein badly enough that you're losing muscle, not fat.

3. Myth bust

Script

[COMMON ADVICE] is why [WHO] stay stuck.

Here's what actually happens: [THE MECHANISM, IN ONE SENTENCE].

What works instead is [YOUR APPROACH, IN ONE SENTENCE] — and it takes [LESS TIME / LESS RESTRICTION] than what you're doing now.

Send [KEYWORD] if you want the detail.

"Eat less, move more" is why busy professionals stay stuck. Here's what actually happens: you cut 800 calories, your body cuts your daily movement by about the same, and you stall at a lower intake than you started with. What works instead is eating more while training in a way that makes your body want to keep the muscle.

4. The callout

Cheap conversations, loose qualification. Good for volume, pair it with tight DM screening.

Script

This is for you if:

• You're [SITUATION ONE]
• You've tried [FAILED APPROACH] and it didn't hold
• You have [REALISTIC TIME] a week, not [UNREALISTIC TIME]
• You want [OUTCOME] without [THE THING THEY WON'T DO]

If that's three out of four, send me [KEYWORD].

It isn't for you if you're after [WHAT YOU DON'T DO].

5. The objection pre-handle

Run this as your second ad to anyone who engaged and didn't convert.

Script

"[THEIR EXACT OBJECTION, IN THEIR WORDS]"

I hear it most weeks. It's fair, and here's the honest answer: [THE ANSWER, WITHOUT SELLING].

[CLIENT NAME] said the same thing before starting. [WHAT HAPPENED].

Still unsure? Send [KEYWORD] and ask me directly. I'll tell you if it's not a fit.

"I don't have time to meal prep." I hear it most weeks. It's fair, and here's the honest answer: none of my clients meal prep. We build your plan around what you already eat. Marcus said the same thing before starting — he eats out four nights a week and he's down two trouser sizes.

6. Direct offer

Your best performer with warm audiences and retargeting. Weakest cold.

Script

I'm taking on [NUMBER] new [WHO] this month.

You get [DELIVERABLE ONE], [DELIVERABLE TWO], and [DELIVERABLE THREE].

It's for people who [QUALIFIER]. It's not for people who [DISQUALIFIER].

Send [KEYWORD] and I'll send you the details and what it costs.

The testimonial interview

Ask these seven, in order. Don't script their answers; the unpolished version converts better. Twenty minutes per client, cut to 45 seconds.

Ask exactly this

1. What was going on for you before we started?

2. What had you already tried?

3. What made you finally reach out?

4. What were you worried about before you said yes?

5. What's different now — something specific, not a number?

6. What surprised you?

7. What would you say to someone sitting where you were?

The two that matter

Questions 4 and 5. Question 4 gives you your objection-handling ad in the client's own words. Question 5 gives you the specific, non-numeric result that passes policy review — "I can get through a shift without crashing" rather than "I lost 18lbs."

Lead the cut with their answer to 4 or 5. Never with "I was so sceptical but..."

Three talking-head hooks to film

Film all three, same outfit, same spot

A. "If you're [DOING THE RIGHT THING] and nothing's moving, it's almost always one of three things."

B. "[CLIENT NAME] trains three times a week for forty minutes. Here's what we changed."

C. "The advice to [COMMON ADVICE] is why [WHO] stay stuck. Here's what actually happens."

Rough beats polished. An ad that looks like a post outperforms an ad that looks like an ad, and version one filmed today beats version two filmed never.

Section 09

Path A — DM scripts

From their first message to a booked call. One question at a time, never a wall of text, and no price until you have earned the right to give it.

The rule that beats every script: reply within five minutes. Conversion roughly halves after the first hour. If you cannot answer inside five minutes during your stated hours, you need an auto-reply holding the slot.

Step 1 — The auto-reply

Fires the second they send your keyword. Buys you time without feeling like a bot.

Send immediately

Hey [FIRST NAME] — thanks for reaching out 👋

Quick one so I point you at the right thing: what's the main thing you're trying to sort out right now?

One question. Open-ended. No link, no pitch, no "I'll get back to you." You are starting a conversation, not acknowledging a ticket.

Step 2 — Qualify in three questions

One message at a time. Wait for each answer. Three questions, then you decide.

Question one — the situation

Got it. How long's that been going on?

Question two — what they've tried

And what have you tried so far for it?

Question three — the constraint

Makes sense. Realistically, how many days a week could you train — and roughly how long per session?

What you're listening for

Qualified: a specific problem, a real attempt to fix it, and three or more realistic days a week.

Not qualified: "just browsing", no timeline, under two days a week, or looking for a free plan. Be warm and close it out — see the exit script below.

Step 3 — The transition

The move everyone fumbles. Ask permission, then offer a specific time. Never "here's my link."

The ask

Okay — based on what you've said I think this is fixable, and it's probably not what you'd expect.

Easier to walk you through it than type it. I've got [DAY] at [TIME] or [DAY] at [TIME] — 20 minutes, no pitch, I'll tell you straight if I'm not the right fit. Which works?

Two specific times, not a link. A calendar link asks them to do work and decide alone. Two times asks them to pick. Send the link only after they choose one.

Step 4 — Lock it in

After they pick

Perfect. Here's the link to lock it in: [BOOKING LINK]

Takes 30 seconds. Use the phone number you actually answer — I'll call that one.

If they book

Immediately

You're in for [DAY, TIME] 🙌

One thing before we talk: have a think about what you'd want to be different in 90 days. Doesn't need to be a number — just what you'd notice. That's where we'll start.

Anything comes up, message me here. Don't just no-show, I'd rather move it.

That last line is worth real money. It gives permission to reschedule, which converts a silent no-show into a kept appointment on another day.

The branches

"How much is it?" — before the call

Answer, don't dodge

Depends what you need — it runs between [LOW] and [HIGH] depending on how much support you want.

I won't quote you properly without knowing your situation, which is what the 20 minutes is for. If it's miles outside your budget I'll say so on the call rather than waste your time.

Refusing to give a range reads as evasive and kills the thread. A range plus a reason keeps it alive.

No reply after your first question

+4 hours

No rush — still want me to take a look at this?

+1 day

Hey [FIRST NAME], didn't want to leave you hanging. Want me to send over what I'd do in your situation, or leave it for now?

+3 days, then stop

All good if the timing's off — I'll leave it here. If it comes back round, just message me. 👍

Three follow-ups, then stop. A fourth costs you more in goodwill than it recovers in bookings.

"I need to think about it"

Reply

Course. What's the bit you're unsure about — whether it'd work for you, or the money?

Never argue. Name the two real options and let them tell you which. You cannot handle an objection you haven't identified.

Not a fit — the exit

Close it warmly

Being straight with you — at [THEIR CONSTRAINT] I don't think I'd be able to get you a result worth what I charge, so I'd rather not take your money.

What I'd do in your position: [ONE GENUINELY USEFUL THING].

If things change, message me.

These people refer. Turning someone down well is marketing.

Three things that break DM conversion

Walls of text. One idea per message. If it needs scrolling, it needs splitting.

Pitching before qualifying. Three questions, then the ask. Never the other way round.

Leading with the link. The link goes out after they pick a time, never before.

Section 11

Path B — the page and the video

On Path A a conversation does the qualifying. Here the page and the form have to do it alone. Build it in this order.

The page, top to bottom

  1. Headline — the outcome, for a named kind of person
  2. One line of sub-copy — what it is and what it isn't
  3. The video, or three short paragraphs if you won't film one
  4. Button — above the fold, repeated after each section
  5. Three proof blocks — client, situation, what changed
  6. Who it's for / who it's not for — two short lists
  7. What happens on the call — removes the "is this a sales pitch" fear
  8. The form
  9. One line about you — last, not first
One page, one action. No menu, no links out, no "about" tab. Every exit is a booking you paid for and lost.

Headline formulas

Pick one, fill it in

1. How [WHO] are [OUTCOME] in [TIME] — without [THE THING THEY DREAD]

2. For [WHO] who are tired of [THE FAILED PATTERN]

3. [NUMBER] [WHO] have used this to [OUTCOME]. Here's how it works.

How shift-working nurses are getting their energy back in 12 weeks — without 5am starts or giving up wine.

Same compliance rule as the ads: describe your work and your clients, never the reader's body.

The video script

Four to six minutes. Filmed on a phone. Seven beats, in this order — the order is doing most of the work.

Beat 1 — Callout · 0:00–0:15

If you're [SITUATION] and [THE THING THAT ISN'T WORKING], this is for you. Give me four minutes and I'll show you why it's happening and what to do instead.

Beat 2 — Who you are · 0:15–0:35

I'm [NAME]. I coach [WHO]. Over the last [TIME] I've worked with [NUMBER] of them, and the same thing comes up almost every time.

Beat 3 — The real problem · 0:35–1:35

Most [WHO] think the problem is [WHAT THEY BLAME]. It isn't.

What's actually happening is [THE MECHANISM, PLAINLY]. Which is why [THE THING THEY KEEP TRYING] makes it worse, not better.

Beat 4 — What works instead · 1:35–2:35

What works is [YOUR APPROACH IN ONE SENTENCE].

Three parts: [ONE], [TWO], [THREE]. That's it. It takes [REALISTIC TIME] a week.

Beat 5 — Proof · 2:35–3:20

[CLIENT] came to me [THEIR SITUATION]. We did exactly that. [WHAT CHANGED, SPECIFICALLY].

Same with [CLIENT TWO] — [ONE LINE].

Beat 6 — The call · 3:20–3:50

If you want me to map that out for your situation, book a call below. It's 20 minutes. I'll ask what you've tried, what's in the way, and what you want different in 90 days — then tell you what I'd do.

No presentation, no pressure. If I'm not the right fit I'll say so and point you somewhere better.

Beat 7 — Who it isn't for · 3:50–4:10

One thing — this isn't for everyone. If you're after [WHAT YOU DON'T DO], I'm not your person. But if [THE QUALIFIER], fill the form in and let's talk.

Beat 7 is the one people cut, and it's the one that works

Disqualifying out loud raises bookings among the people who do qualify, and it raises show rate, because anyone who books has just self-selected against your own stated exclusions.

It also makes the call easier. They arrive having already agreed they're the right fit.

The budget question is not optional

On Path A you find out someone's budget in the DM. On Path B, if you don't ask here, you find out twenty minutes into a call you paid $140 for.

The pre-call question

Notice it appears five times: on the confirmation page, in the email, in the DM, an hour before, and as the opening of the call itself.

It does two jobs. It gives them a reason to show that isn't about you, and it means they arrive having already pictured the outcome — which makes the call shorter and the close easier.

Never ask for homework that takes effort. A form to fill in or a food diary lowers show rate. One question they can answer in the shower raises it.

  • Second call at +8 minutes, not just a text
  • Pre-call question appears in all five places
  • Cancel link disabled, reschedule link live
