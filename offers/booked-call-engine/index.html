Section One

Start here: your ceiling

Before you spend a dollar, work out the most you can afford to pay for one booked call. If you skip this, everything downstream is guesswork.

Ceiling = Show % × Close % × (Client value ÷ 3)

The divide-by-three is the rule that keeps you solvent: a client has to be worth at least three times what you pay to acquire them. Anything tighter and one refund or one bad month wipes the account.

Fill this in

A — What one client pays you in total$________
B — Of people who book, how many show up_____ %
C — Of people who show, how many buy_____ %
D — Your ceiling: B × C × (A ÷ 3)$________

Don't have B and C? Use 70% and 30%. Those are competent-operator numbers, not best case. If your real numbers come in lower, your ceiling drops with them.

That first row is the one to sit with. A $150 month-to-month offer can afford $32 per booked call. Nothing you could buy comes close, which is why no amount of ad skill rescues it — the structure has to change first.

All five assume 70% show and 30% close. Change those and every number moves.

The go / no-go

If your ceiling is under $150, stop. Do not run ads yet.

$150 is roughly the cheapest a booked call realistically gets in this market, with a little headroom. Below it you are blocked against even the best traffic you could buy, and no targeting, creative or budget decision rescues that.

Fix the offer instead. Raise the price, extend the commitment, or add an upfront payment. Then recalculate. This is not a delay — it is the only move that works.

If you need to raise your ceiling

Four levers, in the order they are easiest to move:

  1. Add an upfront payment. $1,500 down then monthly beats pure monthly on day-one cash and on retention.
  2. Extend the minimum term. Four months instead of month-to-month. Body composition results take 12–16 weeks; a one-month offer promises something it can't deliver.
  3. Raise the price. The fastest lever and the one most coaches avoid. A 20% increase moves your ceiling 20%.
  4. Fix show rate before close rate. Easier to move, and Section 9 is entirely about it.

Don't model retention past 12 months. You can't forecast it honestly, and building a budget on it is how accounts go broke slowly.

Section Three

Tracking

Do this before you write a single ad. An account that learns from day one is worth more than a prettier ad, and an account that was never instrumented can never tell you what a call costs.

Five things, in this order. Each has a test. Don't move on until the test passes.

#InstallThe test that proves it
1Pixel on every pageMeta Pixel Helper shows it firing on your offer page
2Domain verifiedGreen tick in Business settings → Brand safety
3Lead or Schedule event on booking confirmationBook a test call yourself. Event appears in Events Manager within 20 minutes
4Conversions APIEvent shows Server as well as Browser in Events Manager
5Event prioritisedYour booking event sits at position 1 in Aggregated Event Measurement
Why the Conversions API is not optional

Browser-only tracking loses a meaningful share of conversions to ad blockers, privacy settings and iOS. Those conversions still happen — Meta just never learns from them, so it keeps optimising on partial data.

Most booking tools (GoHighLevel, Calendly, Hubspot) have a native Meta CAPI integration. Turn it on. It is a settings toggle, not a development project.

Attach a value to the booking

Send your booking event with a value, not bare. Use client value × show % × close % — the expected revenue of one booked call.

Worked example

$2,400 client value × 0.70 show × 0.30 close = $504 per booked call.

Send value: 504 and currency: USD with every Schedule event.

This is what lets Meta report a return figure at all, and it is why most coaches' accounts show nothing in that column. It takes two minutes and almost nobody does it.

The test transaction

  1. Open your offer page in a private window
  2. Book a call using a real email and phone
  3. Open Events Manager → Test events
  4. Confirm the event lands, carries a value, and shows both Browser and Server
  5. Cancel the test booking
Do not skip this. Watching one event land end to end is the only proof that any of the above actually works. Assuming it works is how coaches spend $2,000 before discovering nothing was recorded.
Section Four

Ad scripts

Six frameworks. Replace the bracketed parts and you have a running ad. Build at least eight before you launch — one ad is a guess, eight is a test.

Before you write: the first two seconds carry everything. Never open with "Hey guys" or your own name. Open with the reader's situation.

1. The specific result

Your strongest ad, every time. Works because it is a claim about a named person, not about the reader's body.

Script

[FIRST NAME] is a [AGE]-year-old [JOB] with [CONSTRAINT — two kids, 60-hour weeks, shift work].

Twelve weeks ago she couldn't [SPECIFIC THING SHE COULDN'T DO].

Last week she [SPECIFIC THING SHE DID].

She trains [NUMBER] times a week for [MINUTES] minutes. No [THING THEY FEAR — meal prep Sundays, 5am starts, giving up wine].

I coach [WHO YOU WORK WITH] in [CITY / ONLINE]. If you want the same, send me the word [KEYWORD] and I'll show you what her plan looked like.

Sarah is a 41-year-old nurse with two kids and rotating night shifts. Twelve weeks ago she couldn't get through a shift without three coffees and a crash at 3pm. Last week she ran her first 5k. She trains three times a week for 40 minutes. No 5am starts, no giving up wine. Send me the word ENERGY and I'll show you her plan.

2. Problem, agitate, solve

Script

You're [DOING THE RIGHT THING] and still [NOT GETTING THE RESULT].

So you try harder. [THING THEY ESCALATE TO]. Then [NEXT THING]. Three weeks later you're [WHERE THEY END UP] and quietly wondering if it's just you.

It isn't. It's that [THE ACTUAL MECHANISM THEY'RE MISSING].

I've walked [NUMBER] [WHO] through fixing exactly that. Send [KEYWORD] and I'll tell you where to start.

You're in the gym five days a week and the scale hasn't moved since February. So you add cardio. Then you cut carbs. Three weeks later you're exhausted, eating 1,200 calories, and quietly wondering if it's just you. It isn't. It's that you've been under-eating protein badly enough that you're losing muscle, not fat.

3. Myth bust

Script

[COMMON ADVICE] is why [WHO] stay stuck.

Here's what actually happens: [THE MECHANISM, IN ONE SENTENCE].

What works instead is [YOUR APPROACH, IN ONE SENTENCE] — and it takes [LESS TIME / LESS RESTRICTION] than what you're doing now.

Send [KEYWORD] if you want the detail.

"Eat less, move more" is why busy professionals stay stuck. Here's what actually happens: you cut 800 calories, your body cuts your daily movement by about the same, and you stall at a lower intake than you started with. What works instead is eating more while training in a way that makes your body want to keep the muscle.

4. The callout

Cheap conversations, loose qualification. Good for volume, pair it with tight DM screening.

Script

This is for you if:

• You're [SITUATION ONE]
• You've tried [FAILED APPROACH] and it didn't hold
• You have [REALISTIC TIME] a week, not [UNREALISTIC TIME]
• You want [OUTCOME] without [THE THING THEY WON'T DO]

If that's three out of four, send me [KEYWORD].

It isn't for you if you're after [WHAT YOU DON'T DO].

5. The objection pre-handle

Run this as your second ad to anyone who engaged and didn't convert.

Script

"[THEIR EXACT OBJECTION, IN THEIR WORDS]"

I hear it most weeks. It's fair, and here's the honest answer: [THE ANSWER, WITHOUT SELLING].

[CLIENT NAME] said the same thing before starting. [WHAT HAPPENED].

Still unsure? Send [KEYWORD] and ask me directly. I'll tell you if it's not a fit.

"I don't have time to meal prep." I hear it most weeks. It's fair, and here's the honest answer: none of my clients meal prep. We build your plan around what you already eat. Marcus said the same thing before starting — he eats out four nights a week and he's down two trouser sizes.

6. Direct offer

Your best performer with warm audiences and retargeting. Weakest cold.

Script

I'm taking on [NUMBER] new [WHO] this month.

You get [DELIVERABLE ONE], [DELIVERABLE TWO], and [DELIVERABLE THREE].

It's for people who [QUALIFIER]. It's not for people who [DISQUALIFIER].

Send [KEYWORD] and I'll send you the details and what it costs.

Three hook variants on one body is the cheapest test you will ever run. The hook does most of the work.

The testimonial interview

Ask these seven, in order. Don't script their answers; the unpolished version converts better. Twenty minutes per client, cut to 45 seconds.

Ask exactly this

1. What was going on for you before we started?

2. What had you already tried?

3. What made you finally reach out?

4. What were you worried about before you said yes?

5. What's different now — something specific, not a number?

6. What surprised you?

7. What would you say to someone sitting where you were?

The two that matter

Questions 4 and 5. Question 4 gives you your objection-handling ad in the client's own words. Question 5 gives you the specific, non-numeric result that passes policy review — "I can get through a shift without crashing" rather than "I lost 18lbs."

Lead the cut with their answer to 4 or 5. Never with "I was so sceptical but..."

Three talking-head hooks to film

Film all three, same outfit, same spot

A. "If you're [DOING THE RIGHT THING] and nothing's moving, it's almost always one of three things."

B. "[CLIENT NAME] trains three times a week for forty minutes. Here's what we changed."

C. "The advice to [COMMON ADVICE] is why [WHO] stay stuck. Here's what actually happens."

Rough beats polished. An ad that looks like a post outperforms an ad that looks like an ad, and version one filmed today beats version two filmed never.

Section Seven

DM scripts

From their first message to a booked call. One question at a time, never a wall of text, and no price until you have earned the right to give it.

The rule that beats every script: reply within five minutes. Conversion roughly halves after the first hour. If you cannot answer inside five minutes during your stated hours, you need an auto-reply holding the slot.

Step 1 — The auto-reply

Fires the second they send your keyword. Buys you time without feeling like a bot.

Send immediately

Hey [FIRST NAME] — thanks for reaching out 👋

Quick one so I point you at the right thing: what's the main thing you're trying to sort out right now?

One question. Open-ended. No link, no pitch, no "I'll get back to you." You are starting a conversation, not acknowledging a ticket.

Step 2 — Qualify in three questions

One message at a time. Wait for each answer. Three questions, then you decide.

Question one — the situation

Got it. How long's that been going on?

Question two — what they've tried

And what have you tried so far for it?

Question three — the constraint

Makes sense. Realistically, how many days a week could you train — and roughly how long per session?

What you're listening for

Qualified: a specific problem, a real attempt to fix it, and three or more realistic days a week.

Not qualified: "just browsing", no timeline, under two days a week, or looking for a free plan. Be warm and close it out — see the exit script below.

Step 3 — The transition

The move everyone fumbles. Ask permission, then offer a specific time. Never "here's my link."

The ask

Okay — based on what you've said I think this is fixable, and it's probably not what you'd expect.

Easier to walk you through it than type it. I've got [DAY] at [TIME] or [DAY] at [TIME] — 20 minutes, no pitch, I'll tell you straight if I'm not the right fit. Which works?

Two specific times, not a link. A calendar link asks them to do work and decide alone. Two times asks them to pick. Send the link only after they choose one.

Step 4 — Lock it in

After they pick

Perfect. Here's the link to lock it in: [BOOKING LINK]

Takes 30 seconds. Use the phone number you actually answer — I'll call that one.

If they book

Immediately

You're in for [DAY, TIME] 🙌

One thing before we talk: have a think about what you'd want to be different in 90 days. Doesn't need to be a number — just what you'd notice. That's where we'll start.

Anything comes up, message me here. Don't just no-show, I'd rather move it.

That last line is worth real money. It gives permission to reschedule, which converts a silent no-show into a kept appointment on another day.

The branches

"How much is it?" — before the call

Answer, don't dodge

Depends what you need — it runs between [LOW] and [HIGH] depending on how much support you want.

I won't quote you properly without knowing your situation, which is what the 20 minutes is for. If it's miles outside your budget I'll say so on the call rather than waste your time.

Refusing to give a range reads as evasive and kills the thread. A range plus a reason keeps it alive.

No reply after your first question

+4 hours

No rush — still want me to take a look at this?

+1 day

Hey [FIRST NAME], didn't want to leave you hanging. Want me to send over what I'd do in your situation, or leave it for now?

+3 days, then stop

All good if the timing's off — I'll leave it here. If it comes back round, just message me. 👍

Three follow-ups, then stop. A fourth costs you more in goodwill than it recovers in bookings.

"I need to think about it"

Reply

Course. What's the bit you're unsure about — whether it'd work for you, or the money?

Never argue. Name the two real options and let them tell you which. You cannot handle an objection you haven't identified.

Not a fit — the exit

Close it warmly

Being straight with you — at [THEIR CONSTRAINT] I don't think I'd be able to get you a result worth what I charge, so I'd rather not take your money.

What I'd do in your position: [ONE GENUINELY USEFUL THING].

If things change, message me.

These people refer. Turning someone down well is marketing.

Three things that break DM conversion

Walls of text. One idea per message. If it needs scrolling, it needs splitting.

Pitching before qualifying. Three questions, then the ask. Never the other way round.

Leading with the link. The link goes out after they pick a time, never before.

Why three days, not three weeks

Show rate falls roughly with every day between booking and call. A call booked for next Thursday is a call they have already half forgotten.

A short window feels busy, which is also true — you are only taking four a day.

Reschedule on, cancel off. Someone who can't make it will either move it or vanish. Make moving it the easy option and you keep the appointment instead of losing the lead.

What the booking page must say

  • What actually happens on the call — in one line, plainly
  • How long — and keep to it
  • Who you are — one photo, one sentence of credibility
  • One testimonial — ideally the answer to interview question 4
  • What it is not — "this isn't a hard sell" if that's true
Page copy you can lift

A 20-minute call about your situation.

I'll ask what you've tried, what's getting in the way, and what you want to be different in 90 days. Then I'll tell you what I'd do — and whether I'm the right person to do it with you.

No presentation, no pressure. If it's not a fit I'll say so and point you somewhere better.

Use the phone number you actually answer. I'll call that one.

The confirmation page

Don't waste it on "thanks, you're booked." Give them one job.

Confirmation page copy

You're booked for [DAY, TIME].

Check your phone — I've just sent a text. Reply Y to it so I know you've got it.

Before we talk, have a think about one thing: what would you want to be different in 90 days? Not a number — just what you'd notice day to day.

That reply-Y instruction is the start of the show-up system, and it is the highest-leverage sentence on the page.

The pre-call question

Notice it appears five times: on the confirmation page, in the email, in the DM, an hour before, and as the opening of the call itself.

It does two jobs. It gives them a reason to show that isn't about you, and it means they arrive having already pictured the outcome — which makes the call shorter and the close easier.

Never ask for homework that takes effort. A form to fill in or a food diary lowers show rate. One question they can answer in the shower raises it.

Section Ten

Optimization

What to change, when, and — mostly — when to leave it alone.

When to scale

All three must be true:

  1. Cost per booked call is under your ceiling from Section 1
  2. At least 20 booked calls of data
  3. Show rate above 60%
How to scale

Raise the daily budget 20–30%, then wait 4 days. If cost per call holds, raise again.

Doubling the budget overnight resets learning and usually costs you a week.

The honest read at $600

Six hundred dollars with tracking verified, DMs answered inside five minutes and the reminder system live, and still no booked call, is not an ads problem. It is an offer problem.

Go back to Section 1. Either your ceiling was never high enough, or what you are selling isn't what this audience wants to buy. More budget will not fix either, and will cost you another $600 to confirm.

None of them pay you. They are diagnostic at best — CTR tells you something about a hook when you already know conversations are expensive — and the rest of the time they are a way to feel busy while the only number that matters goes unexamined.

A campaign with a 4% click-through and no booked calls is a failing campaign.

Same day, every week, 15 minutes. Checking daily makes you tamper; checking monthly means you burn three weeks on a broken funnel. Weekly is the rhythm that matches how fast the data actually arrives.

If you're stuck and nothing above fits

Work the funnel backwards, not forwards. Start at the last stage that works and look at the one immediately after it.

Almost every "ads don't work for me" turns out to be one specific stage: no tracking, a slow DM reply, or no show-up system. In that order of likelihood.

The Booked Call Engine — work sections 1, 3, 7 and 9 before you blame the ads. They are where the money is, and they are the four that almost nobody does properly.
